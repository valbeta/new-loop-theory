# On-ramping: The New Loop Theory

## Introduction

The New Loop Theory is an evolving research framework that explores how competitive loops emerge, evolve, and reshape the mechanisms through which value is created and accumulated within competitive systems.

It starts from a fundamental question:

> How does a new competitive loop emerge within an existing system, gain enough momentum, and (possibly) eventually become dominant?

Organizations constantly seek growth, sustainability, and competitive advantage.

Some focus on strengthening existing capabilities.
Some explore new markets and opportunities.
Some attempt to create new business models.

However, behind many significant changes in competitive landscapes lies a deeper mechanism:

A previously less significant actor, capability, or subsystem begins accumulating value through a new reinforcing loop, gradually changing how advantage is created within the system.

The New Loop Theory explores how these new loops emerge, gain momentum, and influence how value accumulates within evolving competitive systems from a systems perspective.

---

# 1. Why This Theory Exists

Organizations operate within competitive systems that continuously evolve.

Over time, companies develop advantages through accumulated capabilities, resources, relationships, and knowledge.

These advantages are rarely created by isolated factors.

They are usually supported by reinforcing mechanisms that allow organizations to continuously create and capture value.

However, history shows that strong organizations can still lose their competitive advantage even when they continue improving. Why?

Sometimes, the conditions that make a competitive advantage valuable can change.

A capability that once created strong leverage may gradually provide less relative value.
A resource that once strengthened a company's position may become less effective under new conditions.
A previously overlooked subsystem may begin attracting increasing value through a different mechanism.

This creates an important question:

> What mechanisms make an existing advantage valuable, and how might those mechanisms evolve over time?

The New Loop Theory provides a perspective for understanding these changes by examining the competitive loops behind value creation.

---

# 2. Competition as a Complex Adaptive System

Traditional business analysis often describes competition through visible elements:

- products;
- customers;
- competitors;
- resources;
- market positions.

The New Loop Theory views competition differently.

A competitive environment is not simply a collection of companies competing against each other.

It is a complex adaptive system shaped by relationships, feedback mechanisms, resource flows, reinforcing loops, and balancing loops.

And in complex adaptive systems, subsystems continuously emerge, adapt, compete, and interact with the broader system.

Some of these subsystems may gradually accumulate more value through reinforcing loops and eventually become influential forces within the competitive system.

Behind every dominant business model lies a system of reinforcing interactions.

What many organizations try to optimize and defend as a seemingly successful business model is often the result of a specific competitive loop operating under certain conditions — conditions that allow a certain subsystem to become dominant.

A competitive system can be represented through interactions such as:

Capability improvement → Customer value increase → Market adoption → Resource accumulation → Further capability improvement

When these relationships reinforce each other, they create a competitive loop.

---

# 3. Competitive Loops: The Mechanism Behind Advantage

A competitive loop is a reinforcing structure that continuously strengthens an organization's ability to create and capture value within a specific competitive environment.

A competitive loop is not:

- a business tactic;
- a growth hack;
- a single competitive advantage.

It is the underlying mechanism that allows value and competitive advantages to accumulate.

For example，a company may benefit from a loop where:

More users → More data → Better product experience → More users

The advantage does not come only from having users or data.

It comes from the reinforcing relationship between them.

Different competitive systems are built around different loops.

A strategy that succeeds in one system may become less effective when the conditions supporting that loop change.

---

# 4. Existing Loops and the Conditions Behind Success

Every successful organization operates within an existing competitive system.

This system contains assumptions about:

- what customers value;
- what capabilities matter;
- what resources create advantage;
- what dimensions define competition.

A dominant business model is often the visible outcome of a competitive loop that has accumulated strength over time.

However, the effectiveness of a loop depends not only on its internal reinforcement, but also on its relationship with the broader competitive system.

When conditions change, the same mechanism that once created advantage may no longer provide the same leverage.

This does not necessarily mean the organization became weaker.

The system that supported its effectiveness may have evolved.

Understanding these conditions is essential for recognizing how competitive systems change.

---

# 5. How New Loops Emerge and Gain Momentum

New competitive loops usually do not appear as immediate replacements for existing systems.

They often begin within the existing system:

- an overlooked customer behavior;
- a new technological possibility;
- a different value creation mechanism;
- a previously underestimated capability.

A relatively insignificant actor or subsystem may begin accumulating value through a new reinforcing loop.

Initially, this loop may appear weaker compared with the dominant system.

However, if the new loop creates stronger reinforcement under emerging conditions, it can gradually gain momentum.

A simplified pattern:

New possibility → Initial advantage → Positive feedback → Growing adoption → New competitive logic

The key question is not simply whether something new appears.

It is how the new loop develops enough reinforcement to shift where value accumulates within the competitive system.

Not every emerging loop becomes dominant.

Some loops may gain momentum and influence specific parts of the system without becoming the primary mechanism of competition.

---

# 6. When Competitive Logic Changes

When a new loop gains sufficient momentum and begins shifting value accumulation patterns, the competitive system may begin to evolve.

This is not only a change in market share or company performance.

It represents a shift in the structure that determines where value accumulates and how new competitive advantages are reinforced.

During this process:

- previously dominant actors may lose relative leverage;
- existing resources may become less strategically valuable;
- new mechanisms may become the foundation of future advantage.

In some cases, a visible consequence of this transition is that existing advantage stocks may gradually shift from being dominant assets to becoming constraints.

The New Loop Theory refers to this phenomenon as a change in the relationship between existing advantages and the evolving competitive system.

---

# 7. Beyond Business Models: From Outcomes to Mechanisms

Business models are among the most commonly used ways to describe successful companies.

They provide an observable representation of how a competitive system creates and captures value.

However, many widely discussed business models are observable outcomes of deeper competitive mechanisms.

A successful business model is often the visible expression of a competitive loop that has effectively accumulated value under specific conditions.

For example, companies may attempt to replicate:

- platform models;
- subscription models;
- ecosystem models.

But copying the visible structure does not necessarily recreate the mechanism that generated its success.

The deeper question is:

> What reinforcing loop made this business model effective within its original competitive system?

Understanding competitive loops helps move from observing successful outcomes to exploring the mechanisms behind them.

---

# 8. Relationship With Existing Theories

The New Loop Theory connects with existing perspectives in innovation, strategy, and systems thinking.

Different theories examine different aspects of how organizations create and sustain value.

- Disruptive Innovation examines how certain innovations can transform markets through different performance dimensions and adoption patterns.
- Blue Ocean Strategy explores how organizations create new competitive spaces.
- First Principles Thinking helps identify fundamental assumptions and constraints.
- Dynamic Capability Theory examines how organizations renew capabilities in changing environments.

The New Loop Theory focuses on another question:

> How do new competitive loops emerge within existing systems, gain momentum, and fundamentally shift where value accumulates?

---

# 9. A Different Lens for Understanding Change

Organizations use many approaches to understand growth and competition.

The New Loop Theory adds another perspective by focusing on the mechanisms behind changing competitive dynamics.

Instead of only asking:

> How can we strengthen our current advantage?

It explores:

> What underlying mechanism has made this advantage valuable, and how might that mechanism evolve?

Instead of only asking:

> How can we discover new opportunities?

It explores:

> Where might new sources of value accumulation be emerging within or around the current system?

Instead of only asking:

> How can we improve our existing business model?

It explores:

> What competitive loop is creating the value behind this business model?

Instead of only asking:

> Why am I getting less when we're all doing the same thing?

It explores:

> What changes in the competitive system are causing value to accumulate differently?

---

# 10. Current Research Status

The New Loop Theory is an evolving research framework.

This repository documents ongoing exploration of:

- theoretical concepts;
- system models;
- business cases;
- empirical observations;
- limitations and open questions.

The purpose is to develop a deeper understanding of how competitive systems evolve, how new loops emerge and gain momentum, how value accumulation patterns shift, and how new mechanisms can sometimes transform the basis of competitive advantage.

It doesn't try to provide a universal formula for innovation.
