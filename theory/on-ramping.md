# On-ramping: The New Loop Theory

*A guided entry into the concepts and questions of The New Loop Theory, aligned with the formal theory through v1.3.1.*

## Introduction

The New Loop Theory is an evolving theoretical research framework concerned with the evolution of Business Systems. It asks why some emerging forces that begin at the edge of a system can eventually change how a Competitive System operates—and why resources, capabilities, and advantages once considered decisive may gradually lose their dominance.

Its central question is:

> **How can a New Loop emerge within an existing Competitive System, develop endogenous reinforcement, and potentially change the system's dominant path of value accumulation?**

The theory approaches this question through the causal structures behind **Value Creation** and **Value Accumulation**. It treats the Business System as a **Complex Adaptive System (CAS)** and takes the **Loop** as its fundamental analytical unit.

This document is an accessible introduction, not a substitute for the formal theory. For the current full statement, see [The New Loop Theory v1.3.1](The-New-Loop-Theory_v1.3.1.md).

## 1. Why Look Beneath Competitive Outcomes?

Business discussions often begin with visible outcomes: product features, technology, customer numbers, market share, brands, or business models. These observations matter, but they do not by themselves explain why an advantage persists—or why it loses its effectiveness.

A resource rarely creates durable advantage in isolation. Its effect depends on how it interacts with other elements in the system, how those interactions feed back into future activity, and which **System Conditions** allow the resulting pattern to continue.

A useful starting question is therefore:

> What causal structure allows this system to create and accumulate value repeatedly?

The New Loop Theory shifts the unit of analysis from isolated firms or assets toward the **loops** connecting capabilities, value creation, adoption, resource accumulation, and further capability development.

## 2. Business Systems and Competitive Loops

A Business System can be understood as a complex adaptive system of actors, resources, relationships, feedback, and changing conditions. Its structure is not fixed: participants adapt, resource flows change, and different subsystems may emerge or gain influence.

A **Competitive Loop** is a reinforcing structure through which an organization or subsystem repeatedly strengthens its ability to create and accumulate value under particular conditions.

For example:

`Capability improvement → Customer value → Adoption → Resource accumulation → Further capability improvement`

The arrows matter. The analytical point is not that a company has capabilities, customers, or resources, but that these elements may form a feedback structure in which one development strengthens the next.

A loop is not simply a tactic, a growth hack, or a list of competitive advantages. It is a causal mechanism. If the relationships or conditions supporting it change, the loop's effectiveness may change as well.

## 3. Old Loops and New Loops

An established Business System is often organized around an **Old Loop**: a familiar pattern of value creation and accumulation that has become influential under prevailing System Conditions.

A **New Loop** may begin within or around the existing system. Its initial source could be a different technology, a previously overlooked behavior, a new capability, or another way of creating value. At first, it may appear marginal when judged through the priorities of the dominant loop.

The important question is not simply whether the new development is novel. It is whether it is building a different causal structure for value accumulation—and whether that structure can become self-reinforcing under the conditions that are emerging.

A New Loop does not automatically replace an Old Loop. It may remain local, fail to reinforce itself, or influence only part of the system. Its eventual significance depends on the mechanism it develops and the system in which it operates.

## 4. The 3A Framework

The theory identifies three fundamental attributes of the New Loop: **Asymmetric, Amplifying, and Ascendant**.

### Asymmetric

**Asymmetry** means a structural difference in how value is created, captured, or accumulated. It is more fundamental than a superficial feature difference. Two competitors may offer similar products while relying on different underlying mechanisms; conversely, visible differentiation may leave the value-accumulation logic unchanged.

The research question is: what structural difference could support a different loop?

### Amplifying

**Amplification** concerns endogenous reinforcement: the loop's own dynamics strengthen its continued development. External funding, market expansion, subsidies, or a favorable temporary condition may support growth, but growth alone does not establish that the loop reinforces itself.

The research question is: what is the feedback mechanism, and how does it strengthen the loop's own capacity to create and accumulate value?

### Ascendant

**Ascendance** is a system-level evolutionary condition in which a loop gains increasing influence over the direction of value accumulation. It concerns the loop's growing systemic significance, not merely a temporary rise in sales or visibility.

The 3A Framework identifies necessary conditions under investigation. It is not a deterministic sequence, a scorecard that guarantees success, or a universal formula for innovation.

## 5. Inside the New Loop: Core Loop and Core Stock

The v1.3.0 theory introduced **Core Loop** and **Core Stock** to examine the internal structure through which a New Loop may become self-reinforcing. Version v1.3.1 clarifies the definition of Core Stock.

### Core Loop

The **Core Loop** is the central feedback structure through which the emerging competitive logic repeatedly reinforces itself. It concerns the causal relationships at the heart of the New Loop, rather than every activity or supporting process around it.

### Core Stock

**Core Stock** is the deeper structural stock accumulated through the Core Loop. It is not necessarily a single, directly measurable quantity. Depending on the system, it may be a compound or structural state produced by several mutually related stocks and conditions.

This distinction matters because the stock that is easiest to measure is not always the stock that best represents the New Loop's underlying structural strength.

### Core Stock and Key Stock

A **Key Stock** generally refers to an explicit stock indicator used to observe, measure, or manage accumulation in a system. It may reveal one aspect of Core Stock, but the two concepts are not interchangeable:

- Key Stock is typically an observable or operationally useful stock indicator.
- Core Stock refers to the deeper structural stock accumulated through the Core Loop.

Core Stock should be examined in relation to the loop that generates it and the conditions under which it supports further reinforcement. External support cannot substitute for endogenous reinforcement. Nor is visible growth, by itself, proof that the Core Loop is self-reinforcing or that Core Stock has formed.

## 6. Asymmetric Iteration: The Core Loop Is Not Fully Designed in Advance

Innovators can deliberately explore potential New Loops around a structural Asymmetry. However, the Core Loop that eventually emerges is not fully determined by the initial design.

It may stabilize through repeated experimentation, feedback from the system, and structural adjustment. What appears to be the original idea may change as actual interactions reveal which relationships can sustain value creation and accumulation.

For this reason, Core Loop formation, Core Stock accumulation, and Amplification should not be treated as a rigid sequence of stages. They may develop concurrently and alter one another through multiple rounds of **Asymmetric Iteration**.

The relevant question is not only whether an initial design looks coherent, but what loop the evolving system is actually producing.

## 7. System Conditions, Gain, and Attractors

A loop operates within a wider system. Its effectiveness depends on **System Conditions**: the relationships, constraints, incentives, resource flows, and other conditions that determine which mechanisms can generate and accumulate value.

A change in those conditions can change the relative effectiveness of competing loops. A loop that once generated strong leverage may become less effective, while a previously peripheral loop may find conditions that allow its reinforcing dynamics to become more consequential.

Research into **Gain Function** and **Attractor Migration** examines this change in the system's dynamics: how shifts in conditions alter which loops can accumulate value and which patterns become influential. These concepts are part of the continuing research agenda and should be interpreted within the formal theory's stated boundaries.

## 8. Stock Migration and Sequential Ascendance

Competitive change is not always adequately described as a contest in which one firm simply defeats another. Resources, capabilities, attention, and accumulated value may shift between different paths of value accumulation.

**Stock Migration** describes this system-level change in the path through which value and resources accumulate. It directs attention to the transition between loops and to the changing role of existing advantages as system conditions evolve.

**Sequential Ascendance** describes the possibility that successive loops gain influence as the system changes over time. It does not imply that every new loop becomes dominant, or that system evolution follows a predictable schedule.

These concepts broaden the analysis from the rise and fall of individual firms toward changes in the mechanisms that organize value accumulation across a Competitive System.

## 9. What the Theory Does—and Does Not—Claim

The New Loop Theory is a framework for investigating the mechanisms behind competitive system evolution. It does not claim that:

- every new technology, product, or business model constitutes a New Loop;
- growth necessarily demonstrates endogenous Amplification;
- every New Loop will become Ascendant;
- an initial innovation design determines the Core Loop that eventually forms;
- Core Stock must always be represented by one measurable variable;
- a successful case, on its own, proves the theory;
- the framework supplies a universal formula for innovation success.

The theory's value depends in part on how clearly it identifies its explanatory boundaries, handles counterexamples, and specifies conditions under which its explanations may fail.

## 10. Reading Cases as Observations, Not Proof

Companion cases such as **NVIDIA CUDA**, **Netflix**, and **Xiaomi** offer material for examining how capabilities, relationships, feedback, and value accumulation interact over time.

A case should not be reduced to a company label attached to a concept. The analysis should ask what the proposed loop is, what stock it accumulates, what feedback could reinforce it, which System Conditions matter, and what alternative explanations remain plausible.

Cases are observations through which the framework can be questioned and refined. They are not, by themselves, empirical proof of the theory.

See the [case-observation collection](../case-observation/).

## 11. Relationship With Other Perspectives

The New Loop Theory intersects with innovation, strategy, and systems thinking. Disruptive Innovation, Blue Ocean Strategy, First Principles Thinking, and Dynamic Capability Theory address related but distinct questions.

The New Loop Theory focuses specifically on how new competitive loops emerge within or around existing systems, how their internal reinforcement and stock accumulation develop, and how the dominant path of value accumulation may change. It does not claim to replace other theories.

## 12. Current Version and Further Research

The current formal public version is **v1.3.1**. Its latest clarification concerns **Core Stock**, including the distinction between Core Stock and Key Stock and the structural character Core Stock may take.

Research continues into:

- identifying and decomposing Core Stock across different systems;
- distinguishing structural Asymmetry from ordinary advantage;
- distinguishing endogenous Amplification from externally supported growth;
- Gain Function and Attractor Migration;
- case coverage, counterexamples, and theoretical boundaries.

**PACT remains an exploratory research direction and is not part of the formal v1.3.1 theory.** Ongoing hypotheses and incomplete frameworks should not be read as established claims of the current version.
