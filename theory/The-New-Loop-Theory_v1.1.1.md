# The New Loop Theory（降维创新理论） V1.1.1

## Abstract

The New Loop Theory is a theoretical research framework concerning the evolution of business systems. It addresses a long-standing question in innovation and strategy: why some emerging forces, originally positioned at the periphery, can ultimately alter the operating mechanisms of the entire competitive system, causing previously decisive advantages—such as enterprises, resources, and capabilities—to gradually lose their dominant role.

Unlike traditional competitive analysis, The New Loop Theory does not initially focus on direct comparisons between enterprises, nor does it attempt to answer "who can defeat whom." Instead, it addresses more fundamental questions: How is value generated, accumulated, and migrated within a business system? What determines long-term competitive outcomes—is it a specific advantage possessed by an enterprise, or the systemic structure capable of continuously generating advantages?

This theory views the business system as a Complex Adaptive System (CAS) and proposes the "Loop" as the fundamental unit of analysis for understanding business system evolution. From this perspective, an enterprise's competitive advantage is not statically embedded in a specific product, technology, resource, or brand. Rather, it is the continuous outcome of a series of feedback relationships under specific systemic conditions.

A mature business system is typically sustained by existing loops. Old loops form stable advantages through resource accumulation, capability precipitation, and organizational adaptation. However, new **value creation** mechanisms may emerge at the periphery of the competitive system, gradually forming a value accumulation logic distinct from the existing system. When a new loop establishes its own reinforcing mechanisms and continuously accumulates momentum, the flow of value within the competitive system may migrate, ultimately leading to changes in the competitive structure.

The New Loop Theory proposes three core conditions to describe the evolutionary capacity of a new loop: **Asymmetry** in the logic of value creation, the **Amplifying** nature of internal dynamic mechanisms, and **Ascendant** state in the direction of system evolution. These three collectively constitute the causal chain from the formation of a new loop to the change in system structure.

**The New Loop Theory** focuses not on how an enterprise defeats the **old loop**, but on how a **new loop** forms, causing the advantages upon which the old loop relies to gradually lose their dominant role in the competitive system.

The current version does not attempt to be a predictive model, nor does it provide specific innovation tools. It focuses on a more fundamental theoretical question:  

> *Under what structural conditions can a new loop form within an existing competitive system and ultimately alter the direction of value accumulation?*

# 1. Problem Origins

## 1.1 Semantic Shift of "Disruptive Innovation" in the Chinese Context

In the development of modern innovation theory, "Disruptive Innovation" is undoubtedly one of the most influential concepts. However, during its dissemination in the Chinese context, the term "颠覆" (disruption/subversion) has gradually undergone a certain degree of semantic shift.

In the daily Chinese context, "颠覆" usually implies overthrowing, defeating, replacing, or even destroying. This linguistic reinforcement leads many innovation practitioners to form a relatively direct imagination of competition: innovation means finding a way to challenge leaders and ultimately defeat existing players.

This understanding tends to shape the innovation process into a direct confrontational relationship:  

> *Entrepreneurs challenging giants, latecomers defeating leaders, and new models replacing old ones.*

If innovation is understood as "finding a way to defeat giants," attention naturally shifts to competitors: where to attack, how to surpass, and how to prove one's product is superior.

This understanding reinforces the confrontational narrative in innovation but weakens the more critical question in the original theory:  

> *Why do some innovations that initially appear marginal, weak, or even inconspicuous ultimately change the entire market structure?*

In fact, many new forces that alter the industrial landscape do not initiate direct frontal competition with the old loop in their existing competitive system. They often establish a different value creation mechanism outside the focus of the old loop, and gradually change the competitive environment as they grow.

Therefore, The New Loop Theory is neither a renaming of "Disruptive Innovation" nor an attempt to replace existing innovation theories. It attempts to address a more fundamental question: If innovation does not mean competitive confrontation, what is the true mechanism driving systemic change?  

A further inquiry is: **What kind of new loop can form, accumulate, and change the system structure itself within an existing competitive system?**

## 1.2 From Competitive Outcomes to System Evolution

Traditional business analysis usually begins with comparisons between enterprises:  

> *Who has stronger technology?*  
> *Who has more users?*  
> *Who has a larger market share?*  
> *Who has higher efficiency?*

These questions focus on competitive outcomes, but the business system is not a static chessboard.

Every decision made by an enterprise alters resource allocation; resource allocation changes organizational capabilities; organizational capabilities affect products and services; products and services influence user behavior; user behavior further impacts revenue, data, brand, and ecosystem. These changes are interconnected and continuously feed back into the system itself.

Therefore, an enterprise's advantage today is likely not brought about by a single isolated factor. What truly deserves attention is: **Why can these factors continuously reinforce each other and sustainably generate advantages?**

From this perspective, the users, brands, channels, technologies, and organizational capabilities possessed by an enterprise are merely stocks within the business system. The more critical question is: Through what loops are these stocks connected, and in what environment can these loops continuously generate returns?

The markets, customers, channels, supply chains, organizational capabilities, technological capabilities, and capital resources faced by a mature enterprise have formed mutually reinforcing relationships after long-term competition. These relationships are not just a set of advantages possessed by the enterprise; together, they constitute the enterprise's way of continuously creating value.

After a value accumulation mechanism has operated for a sufficiently long time, the resource allocation methods, organizational capabilities, and evaluation standards formed around it will increasingly adapt to this mechanism. Thus, a system structure that originally helped an enterprise succeed may gradually restrict the enterprise from discovering another value creation method.

This explains a long-standing phenomenon: successful enterprises are not necessarily the most adept at creating their own substitutes. The problem is not merely that the organization is "not innovative enough," but that the old loop itself is continuously reinforcing the competitive dimensions it is familiar with.

## 1.3 The Problems This Theory Attempts to Explain

The New Loop Theory attempts to explain a specific type of change:  

A new value creation mechanism may initially be small or even outside the mainstream market. It does not immediately defeat the original enterprises, nor does it immediately capture the largest market share. However, it begins to establish its own feedback relationships.

As feedback continues to occur, the new loop gradually acquires more users, data, capabilities, resources, or ecosystem relationships. These newly added stocks further enhance the new loop, endowing it with an increasingly strong self-reinforcing capability.

Meanwhile, systemic conditions are also changing. When these changes accumulate to a certain extent, the advantages that originally supported the old loop may no longer hold the same value. The old loop in the existing competitive system does not necessarily disappear suddenly, but the way it accumulates value begins to change.

The New Loop Theory focuses on this process, along with related questions such as:  

> *Why do some innovations that initially appear merely as marginal changes gradually form their own growth mechanisms?*  
> *Why does having users for a new product not necessarily mean it has established a new competitive logic?*  
> *Why might the advantages accumulated by an old loop suddenly lose their original value after systemic conditions change?*  
> *And the more critical question: Under what conditions can a new local loop develop into the new ascendant loop of the system?*

These questions and changes collectively constitute the research object of The New Loop Theory.

# 2. Core Propositions

## 2.1 The Business System as a Complex Adaptive System

The business system is not a simple combination of static variables. Enterprises, users, suppliers, channels, technologies, capital, and institutions interact continuously, and every action alters the future behavioral conditions of other participants.

Therefore, the outcome of a business system is often not determined directly by a single factor, but is the result of the long-term interaction of multiple feedback relationships.

From the perspective of a complex adaptive system, competitive advantage is no longer just an attribute "possessed" by an enterprise. It is more like a result continuously produced by the system under specific conditions.

Enterprises possess technological capabilities, brands, users, channels, or data, all of which belong to stocks; but how much value these stocks can generate depends on the systemic conditions they are in and the loops through which they are connected.

## 2.2 Old Loops and New Loops

An old loop is the value creation and accumulation mechanism that currently occupies a dominant position in the system. It is usually composed of a series of mutually reinforcing causal relationships and forms a stable resource allocation method through long-term competition.

A new loop, on the other hand, is a value creation and accumulation mechanism that is structurally different from the old loop. It can start from the periphery of the existing competitive system or from a neglected condition within the existing competitive system itself.

A new loop does not initially need to be "larger" than the old loop. What truly matters is that it begins to generate value according to different causal relationships and forms its own accumulation mechanism within these different causal relationships.

Therefore, the comparison between new and old loops should not first fall on product functions or single advantages, but on the level of how value is created, how feedbacks are formed, and how they are continuously accumulated.

## 2.3 The Meaning of System Substitution

The "substitution" mentioned in The New Loop Theory does not mean that old enterprises, old products, or old capabilities must disappear.

An old loop can continue to exist and even retain commercial value for a long time. What truly changes is the way this old loop generates value within the system.

When a new loop continuously changes systemic conditions, the original stocks of the old loop may still exist, but the value they create is different from the past. Thus, the old loop loses its original reinforcing feedback, while the new loop begins to become a more stable path for value accumulation.

Therefore, substitution primarily manifests as a change in the dominant path of value accumulation, rather than a simple change in market share.

## 2.4 Core Definition

The New Loop Theory studies: How new value creation and accumulation loops form within existing business systems, and through self-reinforcing, stock accumulation, and changes in systemic conditions, ultimately may become the new dominant value accumulation path of the competitive system.

# 3. Naming the Theory

The Chinese name of the theory is “降维创新.” The English name is The New Loop Theory. These are not literal translations of one another. Each name reflects a different aspect of the same theoretical proposition and is deliberately retained in its respective language.

In it's Chinese name, **"降维 (dimensionality reduction)"** refers to a shift in the level at which an innovation problem is observed. Rather than remaining within the visible dimensions of competition, the analysis moves toward the underlying structures and value relationships that generate those dimensions.

Accordingly, "dimensionality reduction" corresponds to a specific path of observation:

> *Events → Patterns → Structures → Mental Models*

One descends from events to structures, and from structures one seeks new possibilities. Truly significant opportunities for innovation sometimes lie not in the dimension over which current competitors are contending, but in the deeper structures from which that dimension of competition itself emerges.

**The New Loop Theory** takes its English name from the central object of inquiry: the emergence and evolution of new value-accumulation loops within existing competitive systems. “New” does not imply that every element of a New Loop must itself be new. Existing stocks, technologies, capabilities, or resources may be reorganized within a different value relationship and thereby participate in a different accumulation mechanism.

The two names therefore emphasize different dimensions of the theory. “降维创新” emphasizes the shift in perspective required to recognize a different underlying structure; The New Loop Theory emphasizes the new value-accumulation mechanism that may emerge from that shift. Neither name is intended as a literal translation of the other.

This deliberate difference in naming also reflects a concern informed by the history of “Disruptive Innovation.” When a theoretical concept enters a different linguistic and cultural context, its translated name can acquire semantic associations that differ from the concept's original theoretical meaning. Over time, those associations may influence how the concept is understood, applied, and further developed. The distinction between “降维创新” and The New Loop Theory is therefore intentional: it is designed to reduce the risk that the theory's name itself becomes a source of semantic drift.

# 4. Loops: The Basic Unit of Theoretical Analysis

## 4.1 What is a Loop

In this theory, a loop is not a rhetorical device to describe "circulation."

It refers to a set of causal relationships in which variables influence one another and form feedback loops. When a certain outcome can in turn affect the conditions that produced it, further enhancing or inhibiting the original change, the system has formed a feedback.

Therefore, the key to a loop is not "having many factors," but the existence of a continuous causal closed-loop among these factors.

From this perspective, an enterprise's competitive advantage can be re-understood: advantage is not just the resources an enterprise possesses, but the loop formed among certain resources, behaviors, and outcomes that can continuously reinforce value creation.

## 4.2 Reinforcing Loops

Reinforcing loops cause changes in the system to continue expanding in the same direction.

For example, more users bring more feedback, feedback helps improve the product, product improvement enhances user value, thereby attracting more users. What truly matters here is not "user growth," but the mutually reinforcing causal relationship between user growth and product improvement.

Therefore, growth itself cannot prove the existence of a reinforcing loop.

An enterprise can achieve rapid growth through capital investment, price subsidies, or external resources, but if this growth does not form an internal feedback structure that can in turn enhance itself, then this growth does not equal self-amplification.

## 4.3 Balancing Loops

Balancing loops counteract changes in the system, driving it towards a certain stable state.

A large number of processes, rules, risk controls, resource allocations, and evaluation mechanisms in mature organizations may serve this function. They help the organization maintain stability and help the system maintain stability over time.

However, balancing loops are not the only source of organizational rigidity. Resource specialization, path dependence, existing interest relationships, organizational structures, and evaluation systems can all further reinforce the old loops.

Therefore, understanding the stability of old loops requires placing these factors back into the overall system for observation.

## 4.4 Stocks

Stocks are the states or resources accumulated by the system through past activities, which can influence future system behavior.

Users, data, brands, technological capabilities, channels, organizational capabilities, ecosystem partners, and capital resources can all become stocks.

Stocks themselves are not synonymous with competitive advantage. How much value a stock can truly generate needs to be reflected through specific loops.

This is also a key distinction in The New Loop Theory: A new loop does not necessarily require the complete elimination of the stocks already possessed by the old loop. What it may change are the systemic conditions in which these stocks are situated, and the way these stocks generate value.

## 4.5 Gain Function

The same stock may generate completely different value under different systemic conditions.

Therefore, the competitive or value returns that a certain stock can generate under specific systemic conditions can be understood as its gain function.

It can be simply expressed as:  

> *Gain_old(S, C₀) ≠ Gain_old(S, C₁)*

Where S represents the existing stock, and C represents systemic conditions.

This means that a resource originally very powerful for an enterprise does not naturally guarantee it will have the same value in the future. One of the important roles of a new loop is precisely to change systemic conditions, thereby altering the gain functions of the stocks that give the old loop its advantages.

This is a key entry point for understanding "why old advantages become invalid."

## 4.6 Attractors

Complex systems do not develop in all directions limitlessly. After long-term feedback, the system tends to move towards certain relatively stable states or paths, which can be understood using the concept of attractors.

In a business system, an attractor can be understood as the dominant value accumulation path that the system occupies over the long term.

Therefore, the true **Ascendant** of a new loop is not simply "becoming number one in the market," but rather it beginning to become the system's more primary and stable value accumulation path.

This is why the ultimate change The New Loop Theory focuses on is not the sales volume of a certain product, but where the system itself is converging.

# 5. From Comparative Advantage to Comparative Loops

## 5.1 How Advantages are Continuously Produced by the System

Traditional competitive analysis easily understands advantage as a certain attribute possessed by an enterprise, such as cost, brand, technology, channel, or scale.

But if observed from a systemic perspective, these advantages are usually not statically existent.

Scale can bring cost advantages, cost advantages bring more customers, and customers further bring greater scale; users can generate data, data helps improve products, and improvements attract more users.

Advantages are therefore often the result of the continuous operation of a certain loop.

## 5.2 Loop vs. Loop

If competition is merely comparing resources between enterprises, the core issue of innovation becomes "who has more."

But if competition occurs between loops, the problem changes.

A participant with fewer resources, as long as they can establish a different value creation loop, can avoid directly entering the original competitive dimensions. The early scale of a new loop can even be very small, because what it truly needs to establish is its own feedback structure.

Thus, innovation is no longer just about comparing who has stronger old advantages, but begins to compare: Which loop can form stronger value accumulation in the future?

## 5.3 System Dependence of Advantages

The value of an advantage depends on the system it is in.

A stock that is extremely important in the existing competitive system does not mean it will have the same gain under new systemic conditions. Conversely, a resource previously considered unimportant may suddenly have high value under new systemic conditions.

Therefore, a deep opportunity for innovation comes from redefining "what is valuable" and "why it is valuable."

This is also the fundamental reason why a new loop can change the competitive landscape.

# 6. When Old Loops Become Innovation Constraints

## 6.1 Loop Stabilization

After a loop has operated for a long time, it will produce more and more resources and capabilities that match itself.

The organization begins to recruit, evaluate, invest, and allocate resources according to this loop; customers and channels also gradually adapt to this transaction method.

Thus, the old loop is no longer just a value creation mechanism; it begins to shape the entire organization and market.

## 6.2 Rigidity Effects of Balancing Loops

Stable systems need to suppress deviations.

New ideas usually imply uncertainty, resource reallocation, and a decline in short-term metrics; therefore, mature systems will naturally produce a large number of balancing mechanisms.

These mechanisms themselves are not wrong. They are necessary for the organization to maintain stability.

The problem is that when the system needs to explore another loop, the mechanisms originally used to maintain stability may begin to inhibit the formation of the new loop.

## 6.3 Why Successful Systems Struggle to Create Their Own Substitutes

What a successful system is best at is usually continuing to run the loops it has already validated.

It possesses capabilities, resources, processes, and evaluation standards highly matched with the old loops; therefore, any attempt to deviate from the old loops will face higher internal explanation costs.

This means that success itself may produce a structural constraint: the better the system is at optimizing the current loop, the harder it is to establish another loop outside the current loop.

This does not mean that mature enterprises cannot innovate, but it illustrates that another loop often needs to gain growth space at the periphery of the existing system or under a different logic of resource allocation.

# 7. The 3A Constraint Framework

The New Loop Theory uses three conditions to describe the basic constraints a new loop faces from its formation to becoming a systemic force: **Asymmetric, Amplifying, Ascendant.**

They are not three independent labels, but correspond to theoretical problems at three different levels within a set of causal relationships.

## 7.1 Asymmetric

Asymmetry refers to the structural differences between the new loop and the old loop in value creation, revenue formation, or competitive gain functions.

This difference cannot merely come from more functions, lower prices, or better experiences. A product can be excellent yet still operate within the old loop.

True asymmetry needs to answer: If the new loop holds, does it generate and accumulate value in a different way from the old loop?

Therefore, asymmetry is a structural requirement for "newness."

## 7.2 Amplifying

Amplifying refers to the new loop having an endogenous reinforcing mechanism.

A certain outcome in the new loop can in turn reinforce other conditions in the loop, causing the loop to strengthen as it operates.

Therefore, amplifying describes a causal structure, not a growth outcome.

A system that relies on continuous external capital, subsidies, or manual resources to maintain growth can grow very fast, but this itself does not prove it possesses self-amplifying characteristics.

## 7.3 Ascendant

Ascendant describes the outcome at the system level.

When a new loop, through continuous development, begins to change systemic conditions and gradually becomes the system's more primary value accumulation path, it can be said to have entered an ascendant state.

Therefore, **Ascendant** does not simply say "winning market competition," nor is it a static market share indicator, but a manifestation of the change in the system's attractor.

## 7.4 Causal Chain

The three conditions can be understood within a basic dynamic relationship:  

> *Asymmetric → Amplifying → Stock Accumulation → System Condition Change → Attractor Migration → Ascendant*

It needs to be specially emphasized here that 3A is a necessary condition framework, not a success prediction model. Even if a new loop possesses structural differences and self-amplifying capabilities, it may still be affected by time, resources, external environment, competitor reactions, and the influence of other loops.

---

# 8. How a New Loop Becomes the New Dominant Loop

A new loop does not become the dominant loop the moment it appears. It usually undergoes a dynamic process from the periphery to a systemic force.

In the early stage, the new loop may only exist at the periphery of the existing system. Its scale is small, and it lacks sufficient resources to compete head-on with the old loop, but it begins to form value relationships different from the old loop.

As the reinforcing mechanism is established, the new loop begins to self-reinforce. New value further brings new users, resources, data, capabilities, or ecosystem relationships; these newly added stocks in turn improve the new loop's ability to continue operating.

When stocks continuously accumulate, the new loop begins to gain momentum. At this point, the truly important change is not just the increase in scale, but that the new loop gradually begins to influence systemic conditions.

After systemic conditions change, the original stocks of the old loop may still exist, but their gain functions begin to change. Resources that were once effective in reinforcing the old loop may no longer generate the same degree of value.

Thus, the old loop begins to lose its original reinforcing capacity, while the reinforcing capacity of the new loop continues to increase. When this change reaches a certain extent, the system may migrate from the original attractor to a new attractor.

Therefore, a typical dynamic path of a new loop can be described as:  

> *Emergence at the Edge → Establishment of Self-Amplification → Stock Accumulation → Momentum Growth and Shift in Competitive Dimensions → Change in the Gain Function of the Old Loop → Migration of the System Attractor*

This is a typical dynamic evolutionary path, not a linear process that all new loops must strictly follow.

The core mechanism truly worth attention is:  

> **New Loop → New Value Accumulation → System Condition Change → Change in the Gain Function of the Old Loop → Loss of Gain in the Old Loop → Migration of the System Attractor**

Therefore, the new loop does not simply "defeat" the old loop. The deeper change is that the new loop alters the conditions upon which the old loop relies to generate value.

# 9. Core Mechanisms

## 9.1 Stock Migration Mechanism of New Loops

In complex business systems, the formation of a new loop does not always mean starting from scratch to build a completely independent value creation system. In fact, a large number of successful new loops are not born outside the system, but find new value accumulation methods within the existing system and reorganize original resources to serve different value logics.

This means we need to correct a linear innovation assumption that is easy to produce: innovators do not always have to create new resources, but under specific conditions, they can rediscover the value of existing resources in the new system.

After long-term operation, a mature business system will precipitate a large amount of resources. These resources may include user relationships, technological capabilities, brand trust, supply chain networks, transaction data, or organizational experience. They initially served the old loop, so their value was defined by the old loop's gain function. But when a new value creation mechanism emerges, some existing resources may detach from the original cycle and enter a new value creation structure.

This phenomenon is called **Stock Migration** in The New Loop Theory.

Stock migration refers to resources already formed in the existing system being reorganized under a new value creation logic and entering a new value cycle.

The key to stock migration is not "having more resources," but whether resources can change the possibilities for the formation and development of the new loop.

### 9.1.1 From Resource Inheritance to Value Reconstruction

Traditional competitive analysis often focuses on how many resources an enterprise has and whether these resources constitute a competitive advantage. But in a dynamic business system, resources themselves do not directly produce advantages. The same resource may generate completely different value in different loops.

For example, a platform with a large number of users can use user scale for ad distribution, or for transaction matchmaking, content recommendation, or ecosystem building. User scale itself does not determine the final value; what truly determines the competitive result is: into which feedback structure are these resources connected?

Therefore, stock migration is not asset transfer in a simple sense (old business resources → new business replication), but rather:  

> *Existing Stocks → Reorganization → New Value Cycle*

Only when resources enter a new cycle and change the way value is accumulated in the cycle is true migration completed.

This is also why many enterprises have massive historical accumulation but cannot complete the second growth curve. They have resources but cannot change the role of resources in the system; they inherit past advantages but cannot transform these advantages into new growth drivers.

### 9.1.2 Stock Migration and the Formation of New Loops

In the process of new loop formation, stock migration provides an important path: innovators do not necessarily need to challenge the existing dominant players head-on in the entire business system, but can find value nodes that have not been fully released in the existing competitive system. This reduces the cost of establishing the new loop in the early stage.

A completely new loop starting from scratch needs to simultaneously complete: value logic validation, user relationship establishment, resource accumulation, and market education. Stock migration allows innovators to leverage part of the accumulation in the existing system to accelerate the formation of the new loop.

However, it should be noted that stock migration does not mean that a new business inheriting the advantages of the old loop will definitely succeed. If the migrated resources still operate in the way of the old loop, then it is just business expansion, not the formation of a new loop.

True stock migration must satisfy a core condition: the migrated resources must serve the new direction of value accumulation, rather than continuing to reinforce the old loop.

### 9.1.3 Case Observation: Xiaomi - From MIUI to the Ecosystem

> ***Nature of Case Observation:** This case is presented solely as an illustrative observation of the underlying theoretical mechanisms, intended to concretize the abstract concepts within the New Loop Theory. The case details are drawn from publicly available business histories and industry consensus, and should not be construed as rigorous business historiography or empirical academic research.*

Xiaomi's early development process embodies a typical form of stock migration. In the early stage of the smartphone industry, the competitive loop of traditional mobile phone manufacturers mainly revolved around hardware capabilities:  

> **Product RD → Channel Sales → Phone Sales → Next-generation Product Investment**

In this loop, users were more the endpoint of product sales, rather than co-participants in the product iteration process.

Xiaomi did not initially challenge the entire mobile phone industry chain directly, but sought a new value accumulation entry point within the Android ecosystem. The MIUI community connected user feedback, product iteration, and user participation, transforming user relationships from one-time transactional relationships to continuous participatory relationships.

In this process, Xiaomi did not create the resource of "user feedback," nor did it create the Android ecosystem itself. Instead, it reorganized a node in the existing ecosystem to form a new cycle:  

> **User Participation → Product Feedback → System Optimization → User Growth → More User Participation**

When this cycle gradually matured, it further migrated into a larger commercial system, connecting with mobile phone hardware, IoT devices, and ecosystem chain enterprises to form a more complex value network. The Xiaomi case illustrates that a new loop does not necessarily have to enter from outside the old loop system. An organization can cultivate new growth directions within the existing competitive system by redefining the value roles of existing resources.

### 9.1.4 Theoretical Significance of Stock Migration

Stock migration supplements The New Loop Theory's understanding of "newness."

The "new" in a new loop does not mean that all constituent elements must be new. What truly matters is: Has the value relationship changed?

A new loop may use users, technologies, channels, or even infrastructure from the existing competitive system, but if these resources are reconnected and enter a different feedback structure, it may still form a new value creation logic.

Therefore, to judge whether an innovation belongs to a new loop, one cannot simply observe whether it has established new stock resources; it is also necessary to observe: Have existing stock resources been reorganized and served for different value accumulation directions?

Stock migration explains why some enterprises can achieve breakthroughs in mature markets, and also explains why some enterprises with huge resource advantages cannot establish new growth cycles: resources do not migrate automatically; only when resources enter a new loop can they generate new value.

## 9.2 Sequential Ascendance

In complex business systems, a dominant position is not a permanent state.

In [v1.0](theory/The-New-Loop-Theory_v1.0.md), Ascendant was used to describe the stage where a new loop accumulates enough momentum and begins to change the system's gravitational field. But as the system continues to evolve, a new question arises: 

**If a new loop has successfully changed the competitive landscape, will it permanently maintain its dominance?**

The answer given by real-world business systems is no.

The business system is not a static environment. The formation of every new loop will change the behavior of participants, the direction of resource flow, and the path of value accumulation. And these changes will create new opportunity spaces, making it possible for another loop to form around a new value logic and challenge the original dominant loop.

Therefore, Ascendant is not the final state, but a stage where a loop becomes the main attractor under specific systemic conditions and time windows.

Sequential Ascendance describes: In a complex business system, different value accumulation loops continuously compete around the system's attractor. A new loop that has formed dominance may still have its dominant position replaced by another new loop that changes the direction of value accumulation.

### 9.2.1 From "Substitution Relationship" to "Attractor Competition"

Traditional innovation narratives often adopt a linear substitution model: Old technology has problems → New technology appears → New technology replaces old technology.

This model is applicable to some technology substitution scenarios but cannot explain the long-term evolution in complex business systems. In the real market, a new loop does not simply defeat the old loop, but changes: what resources are worth accumulating, and what feedback structures can generate higher value.

Therefore, the rise of a new loop does not mean the old loop disappears immediately. The old loop may continue to exist, but its attractiveness declines; at the same time, the new loop gradually changes the flow of resources in the system through new value accumulation methods. The evolution of the business system is closer to:

> *Old loop forms dominance → New loop appears → Two value accumulation methods compete → System conditions change → New loop becomes the new attractor*

This process can be repeated. Therefore, innovation competition is not a one-time victory or defeat, but a process of sequential ascendance of multiple loops on different time scales.

### 9.2.2 Sequential Ascendance and Changes in System Conditions

An important question is:

**Why will a once successful new loop eventually lose its dominance?**

The answer is usually not because it suddenly loses value, but because systemic conditions have changed, making its original advantages no longer have the same gain capacity. The competitive advantage of a new loop comes from its matching relationship with the systemic environment at that time. When the environment changes, conditions such as user behavior, technological foundation, cost structure, and industrial connection methods may all change.

Advantages in the original loop may decrease, or even transform into new limitations. Therefore, sequential ascendance is not simply "latecomers defeating pioneers," but new systemic conditions creating new directions for value accumulation.

### 9.2.3 Case Observations:

> ***Nature of Case Observation:** This case is presented solely as an illustrative observation of the underlying theoretical mechanisms, intended to concretize the abstract concepts within the New Loop Theory. The case details are drawn from publicly available business histories and industry consensus, and should not be construed as rigorous business historiography or empirical academic research.*

#### BlackBerry

---

The development process of BlackBerry embodies a typical form of sequential ascendance.

In the early stage of the smartphone industry, traditional mobile phone manufacturers mainly established value cycles around communication functions and hardware sales. BlackBerry, however, constructed a different value accumulation logic:

> **Enterprise mobile needs → Secure communication capabilities → Enterprise deployment → More enterprise users → Stronger enterprise communication ecosystem**

BlackBerry's core competitiveness was not mere mobile phone hardware, but enterprise mobile communication infrastructure. Under the systemic conditions at that time, this loop formed a clear attraction: enterprise customers gained security and efficiency improvements, organizational IT departments formed procurement needs, and more enterprise applications developed around this system.

Therefore, although BlackBerry's loop did not continue to be the dominant attractor in the subsequent touch-screen era, it once established a truly dominant value loop.

Subsequently, the development of the mobile Internet changed the direction of system competition. Smartphones gradually transformed from "enterprise communication devices" to "general-purpose computing platforms." The new value accumulation direction revolved around:

> **Application ecosystem → Developer participation → User growth → More applications**

Apple and Android did not simply provide better phones, but redefined the most worth-accumulating resources in mobile devices.

Under the new systemic conditions, the importance of BlackBerry's original advantages declined, and its closed secure system and dedicated ecosystem no longer possessed the same growth momentum. Therefore, BlackBerry's decline cannot be simply explained as "innovation failure," but an attractor migration in the process of sequential ascendance: the enterprise mobile communication loop once occupied dominance, and subsequently, the general-purpose mobile computing ecosystem became the new dominant direction.

#### Skype

---

Skype also demonstrates the mechanism of sequential ascendance.

In the early days of Internet communication, traditional international communication relied on operator infrastructure and generated high costs through distance. Skype established a new value cycle through Internet technology:

> **More users → Richer communication network → Increased communication value → More users joining**

It changed the way communication value was formed, transforming cross-regional communication from a high-cost service to an Internet infrastructure capability.

But as time went on, communication needs changed. Users and enterprises no longer just needed "low-cost communication," but increasingly needed stable video conferencing experiences, enterprise collaboration capabilities, multi-person interaction, organizational management capabilities, etc. New competitive loops began to form around enterprise collaboration scenarios.

Zoom's growth logic was not simply copying Skype, but redefining value accumulation in the new demand structure:

> **Enterprise collaboration needs → More stable meeting experience → Enterprise adoption → More organizational scenario validation → Enhanced product capabilities**

Skype once changed the communication system, but the new loop changed "how communication value should be accumulated."

This is precisely the core meaning of sequential ascendance.

### 9.2.5 Supplement to The New Loop Theory by Sequential Ascendance

After introducing sequential ascendance, the meaning of "Ascendant" in the 3A framework needs to be re-understood.

A new loop becoming dominant does not mean it gains a permanent advantage. It means: **Within the current system boundaries and time window, the loop becomes the main direction of value accumulation.**

As new loops appear, the system may change again. Therefore, judging whether a new loop is successful requires distinguishing two questions:

- First, has it formed the dominant attractor in the current stage?
- Second, can it continuously face future changes in systemic conditions?

The first question belongs to **Ascendant**, and the second question belongs to the long-term evolution in the process of sequential ascendance.

Sequential ascendance does not negate **Ascendant**, but places **Ascendant** back into the complex system for understanding: dominance is not the endpoint, but the starting point for the next round of competition.

# 10. First Principles and New Loops

## 10.1 What First Principles Answer

First principles help us return to more basic facts of things and re-examine those assumptions that have been taken for granted in a field.

It can help innovators discover that the product forms, business models, and competition methods we take for granted today are not necessarily the inevitable results of the problem itself.

But discovering underlying facts does not automatically mean discovering a new business loop.

First principles help answer what is fundamentally possible, while The New Loop Theory further asks: "Based on these underlying facts, can a different value creation and accumulation mechanism be formed?"

## 10.2 From Essence to Loops

Starting from first principles, innovators may discover an underlying capability that was previously overlooked.

But only when this capability is embedded in new causal relationships and begins to generate continuous feedback can it become part of a new loop.

Therefore, first principles can be an important entry point for discovering new loops, but they cannot replace the new loop itself.

## 10.3 NVIDIA CUDA

The case details are drawn from publicly available business histories and industry consensus, and should not be construed as rigorous business historiography or empirical academic research.*

GPUs originally possessed highly parallel computing capabilities. One of the truly important changes was that NVIDIA, through CUDA, turned this computing capability into a general-purpose computing foundation that is easier for developers to use and program, and gradually formed a continuous relationship among developers, software ecosystems, application demands, and computing capabilities.

Two different levels of questions can be seen here. Recognizing the parallel computing capability of GPUs is a re-recognition of underlying capabilities; while the developer and software ecosystem built around CUDA has enabled this capability to enter a new value creation and accumulation relationship.

Therefore, what this case demonstrates is not "first principles equal new loops," but how underlying facts become the foundation for the formation of new loops.

# 11. Unified Explanatory Framework for Cases

> ***Nature of Case Observation:** This case is presented solely as an illustrative observation of the underlying theoretical mechanisms, intended to concretize the abstract concepts within the New Loop Theory. The case details are drawn from publicly available business histories and industry consensus, and should not be construed as rigorous business historiography or empirical academic research.*

## 11.1 Netflix

The value creation of traditional physical rental business formed a stable relationship with stores, inventory, geographical location, and user in-store behavior.

Netflix's early DVD-by-mail model began to establish another value relationship: users gained larger selection space and higher convenience; as subscribed users increased, scale was formed; scale further improved service and cost structure, thereby reinforcing user value.

This new loop did not initially need to immediately eliminate physical stores. The real change was that the way users obtained movie content and the way the enterprise accumulated value began to differ.

Therefore, the importance of the Netflix case lies not just in "changing from offline to online," but that after a new value loop was gradually established, the systemic conditions where original stocks such as stores, inventory, and geographical location were situated changed.

It should be noted that DVD-by-mail and the subsequent streaming can be regarded as the loop evolution of Netflix at different stages, and should not be simply compressed into an undifferentiated "Netflix new loop."

## 11.2 Xiaomi

The smartphone market itself is not a simple linear competitive market. Traditional manufacturers already possess complex loops such as R&D, supply chain, channels, brand, and manufacturing capabilities.

One of the important changes for Xiaomi was embedding user participation, software iteration, community feedback, and ecosystem expansion more deeply into the product value creation process.

User feedback helped product iteration; product and service improvements enhanced user relationships; user scale and ecosystem expansion further created new value spaces.

Therefore, what is truly worth observing from the perspective of The New Loop Theory in the Xiaomi case is not a single product function, but how it reorganized the feedback relationships among users, products, software, and the ecosystem.

# 12. New Loops and Moats

Moats are usually used to describe an enterprise's ability to maintain a competitive advantage over the long term.

From the perspective of The New Loop Theory, a moat is not a static asset unrelated to loops. Truly powerful moats often come from stocks formed after long-term operation and the mutual reinforcement among these stocks.

Users, data, brands, technological capabilities, channels, and ecosystem partners can all become part of a moat, but the reason they form protection is that they are embedded in a continuously operating value loop.

This also means that moats are not permanently unchanging.

When a new loop changes systemic conditions, the stocks corresponding to the old moat may still exist, but their gain functions change. Resources that could form strong protection in the past may no longer have the same value in the new system.

From this perspective, what truly needs attention is not "whether an enterprise has a moat," but: **Which loop is this moat attached to, and are the systemic conditions of this loop changing?**

# 13. Theoretical Boundaries

The New Loop Theory must first know its theoretical boundaries.

Not all innovations belong to the "new loops" described by The New Loop Theory. A product can be better, faster, cheaper, have brand new functions, or bring significant user growth, but as long as it still operates within the original value creation and accumulation logic, it cannot be classified into a new loop merely based on being "new."

## 13.1 Compared with Incremental Innovation

Incremental innovation is usually continuous improvement on existing products, capabilities, and competitive logic. It can be extremely valuable and produce huge business results.

But if the improvement does not form a different value creation and accumulation loop, it still belongs to innovation within the old loop.

The New Loop Theory focuses on changes in loop structure, not on how large the magnitude of change is.

## 13.2 What is Not The New Loop

New technologies, new products, new functions, new business models, or even rapid growth cannot independently prove that the "new loop" focused on by The New Loop Theory is established.

The key to judgment remains: Whether a structurally different value creation and accumulation mechanism has been formed, and whether it has begun to establish its own feedback relationships.

## 13.3 Why Not All New Products are New Loops

A product is a result that users can directly observe, while a loop describes the systemic relationship that produces and accumulates this result.

Two products can have completely different appearances yet operate in the same loop; the same product may also gradually enter another loop as the business model and systemic conditions change.

Therefore, product innovation and new loop innovation are not concepts on the same level.

## 13.4 Why Growth Does Not Mean Ascendant

Growth only describes changes in scale.

**Ascendant** in the 3A framework describes the change in the system's dominant value accumulation path. A new product can grow at high speed yet still be attached to the old loop; a business may also grow very fast in a local market but has not formed a force capable of changing the system's attractor.

Therefore, growth itself is insufficient to prove **Ascendant**.

## 13.5 Why Subsidized Growth Does Not Mean Amplifying

External capital, price subsidies, resource tilt, or artificial promotion can all manufacture growth.

**Amplifying** in the 3A framework focuses on whether there is a feedback structure within the new loop that can continuously reinforce itself.

Therefore, growth driven by external resources cannot be directly used as proof of the existence of a self-amplifying loop.

# 14. Relationship with Related Existing Theories

The New Loop Theory has obvious connections with multiple existing innovation and strategy theories, but the problems studied are not exactly the same.

**Christensen's "Disruptive Innovation"** mainly explains how some new entrants enter from specific market positions and change the competitive structure under specific conditions. The New Loop Theory further focuses on how the value creation loop itself forms, and how the new loop changes systemic conditions.

**First Principles** help innovators return to more underlying facts and assumptions. The New Loop Theory pushes this underlying understanding further forward to value generation and feedback structures.

**Lean Startup** focuses on how to reduce the verification cost of innovation assumptions. The New Loop Theory asks a step earlier: What direction is worth being verified, and what value relationships may form a new loop?

**Design Thinking** emphasizes user needs, experience, and value creation. The New Loop Theory focuses on whether these value relationships can further form different systemic feedback.

**The Second Curve** emphasizes new growth curves outside the existing growth curve. The New Loop Theory attempts to explain the causal structure behind this new curve, and the systemic relationship between the old and new loops.

**Strategy theories** focus on how enterprises choose competitive fields, establish unique positions, and gain long-term advantages. The New Loop Theory pushes the problem further to the loop level where competitive advantages are produced: In what loop is the enterprise competing, and is there another loop worth entering?

These theories can complement each other, but they are not different expressions of the same theoretical problem.

# 15. Areas of Focus and Theoretical Limitations

## 15.1 Not an Organizational Change Theory

The "new loop" focused on by The New Loop Theory may require the organization to change, but The New Loop Theory itself does not explain how the organization should be designed, governed, or how organizational change should be implemented.

The organization is just an important systemic condition in which the new loop operates.

## 15.2 Not a Predictive Model

The New Loop Theory describes a kind of systemic innovation pattern and its possible dynamic evolution, but it is not a predictive model.

Even if a new loop possesses certain theoretical conditions, it cannot be used to calculate when it will succeed, what the probability of success is, or that it will definitely become the market leader.

## 15.3 Cannot Eliminate Uncertainty in Complex Systems

Complex adaptive systems have a large number of interacting and incompletely controllable factors.

New loops can change the system, but innovators cannot completely control how the system responds. Therefore, the theory can help understand the structure, but cannot eliminate uncertainty.

## 15.4 Not a Unified Explanation for All Innovations

The New Loop Theory only explains specific types of structural innovation.

A large number of product optimizations, incremental innovations, technological improvements, operational innovations, and organizational innovations do not need to be explained as new loops.

The value of the theory lies not in covering all innovations, but in accurately explaining the specific class of phenomena it has chosen.

## 15.5 Does Not Guarantee the Success of New Loops

There are multiple stages between the formation, growth, and systemic substitution of a new loop.

Even if a new loop has structural differences and forms a self-amplifying mechanism, it may still fail to become the new dominant path due to insufficient resources, immature timing, changes in the external environment, competitor reactions, or the emergence of other stronger loops.

Therefore, The New Loop Theory explains a possible mechanism of system evolution, not a guarantee of success.
