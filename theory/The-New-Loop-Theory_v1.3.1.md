# The New Loop Theory V1.3.1

[English](The-New-Loop-Theory_v1.3.1.md) | [简体中文](The-New-Loop-Theory_v1.3.1_zh-CN.md)

# Abstract

The New Loop Theory is a theoretical research framework concerning the evolution of Business Systems. It attempts to explain a long-standing question in the fields of innovation and strategy:

Why are some emerging forces that originally exist at the edge of a system eventually able to change the way an entire Competitive System operates, causing resources, capabilities, and advantages that were previously considered decisive to gradually lose their original dominance?

Unlike traditional competitive analysis, The New Loop Theory does not begin with direct comparisons between firms. Instead, it focuses on the causal structures behind **Value Creation** and **Value Accumulation**.

Competitive advantages within a Business System do not exist statically in a particular product, technology, resource, or brand. Rather, they are continuously generated by a series of feedback relationships under specific **System Conditions**.

This theory views the Business System as a **Complex Adaptive System (CAS)** and takes the **Loop** as the fundamental analytical unit for understanding business system evolution.

A mature Business System is usually maintained by an existing Loop, while new forms of value creation may emerge at the edge of an existing Business System and gradually develop a Value Accumulation logic different from that of the Old Loop.

The **New Loop** studied by The New Loop Theory possesses three fundamental attributes:

Structural **Asymmetry**, endogenous **Amplification**, and evolutionary **Ascendance**.

Together, these three attributes constitute the **3A Framework**.

Version 1.3.0 further moves inside the New Loop and introduces **Core Loop** and **Core Stock** to explain what kind of fundamental feedback structure enables a New Loop to accumulate value creation capability, and how such accumulation generates self-reinforcement.

Based on this foundation, this version further introduces **Asymmetric Iteration** as an application mechanism in the evolution of New Loops.

Innovators may proactively design potential New Loops around structural Asymmetry. However, the eventual Core Loop formed is not completely determined by the initial design. Instead, it gradually stabilizes through continuous experimentation, system feedback, and structural adjustment.

Therefore, Version 1.3.0 further clarifies the deeper internal relationship among New Loop, Core Loop, Core Stock, and the 3A Framework.

This relationship should not be understood as a fixed temporal sequence. Core Loop, Core Stock, and Amplification may occur simultaneously through multiple rounds of Asymmetric Iteration and continue to be adjusted and reconfigured.

Only when the accumulation of Core Stock generates continuous endogenous reinforcement and gains sufficient systemic strength under specific System Conditions can a New Loop enter an **Ascendant** state.

# 1. Origins of the Theory

## 1.1 The Semantic Shift of “Disruptive Innovation” in the Chinese Context

During the development of modern innovation theory, **Disruptive Innovation** has undoubtedly become one of the most influential concepts.

However, during its dissemination in the Chinese context, the term “disruption” has gradually undergone a certain degree of semantic shift.

In everyday Chinese usage, “disruption” often implies overthrowing, defeating, replacing, or even destroying something.

This linguistic reinforcement has caused many innovation practitioners to develop a relatively direct competitive imagination:

Innovation means finding a way to challenge leaders and eventually defeat existing players.

This understanding tends to frame innovation as a direct confrontation:

> Entrepreneurs challenge giants, new entrants defeat leaders, and new models replace old models.

If innovation is understood as “finding a way to defeat giants,” attention naturally shifts toward competitors:

> Where can they be attacked?
> How can they be surpassed?
> How can a product prove itself stronger?

This interpretation strengthens the confrontational narrative of innovation while weakening the deeper question contained in the original theory:

**Why can some innovations that initially appear marginal, weak, or insignificant eventually reshape an entire market structure?**

The question worth studying is not: “How do late entrants defeat market leaders?”

If competitive advantage itself originates from a stable Value Creation and Value Accumulation Loop, then changing a competitive structure may not require creating a stronger version of the Old Loop. Instead, it may require the formation of a New Loop with a fundamentally different causal structure.

In fact, many new forces that alter the industrial landscape do not initiate direct frontal competition with the old loops within the existing business system. They often establish a different value creation mechanism outside the focus of the old loops, and gradually change the competitive environment as they grow.

Therefore, The New Loop Theory is neither a renaming of "Disruptive Innovation" nor an attempt to replace existing innovation theories. It attempts to address a more fundamental question:

**What kind of new loop can form, accumulate, and change the system structure itself within an existing competitive system?**

## 1.2 From Competitive Outcomes to System Evolution

Traditional business analysis usually begins with comparisons between enterprises:  

> _Who has stronger technology?_  
> _Who has more users?_
> _Who has a larger market share?_
> _Who has higher efficiency?_

These questions focus on competitive outcomes, but the business system is not a static chessboard.

Every decision made by an enterprise alters resource allocation; resource allocation changes organizational capabilities; organizational capabilities affect products and services; products and services influence user behavior; user behavior further impacts revenue, data, brand, and ecosystem. These changes are interconnected and continuously feed back into the system itself.

Therefore, an enterprise's advantage today is likely not brought about by a single isolated factor.

What truly deserves attention is: **Why can these factors continuously reinforce each other and sustainably generate advantages?**

From this perspective, the users, brands, channels, technologies, and organizational capabilities possessed by an enterprise are merely stocks within the business system. The more critical question is: Through what loops are these stocks connected, and in what environment can these loops continuously generate returns?

The markets, customers, channels, supply chains, organizational capabilities, technological capabilities, and capital resources faced by a mature enterprise have formed mutually reinforcing relationships after long-term competition. These relationships are not just a set of advantages possessed by the enterprise; together, they constitute the enterprise's way of continuously creating value.

After a value accumulation mechanism has operated for a sufficiently long time, the resource allocation methods, organizational capabilities, and evaluation standards formed around it will increasingly adapt to this mechanism. Thus, a system structure that originally helped an enterprise succeed may gradually restrict the enterprise from discovering another value creation method.

This explains a long-standing phenomenon: successful enterprises are not necessarily the most adept at creating their own substitutes. The problem is not merely that the organization is "not innovative enough," but that the old loop itself is continuously reinforcing the competitive dimensions it is familiar with.

## 1.3 The Problems This Theory Attempts to Explain

The New Loop Theory attempts to explain a specific type of change: A new value creation method may initially be small or even outside the mainstream market. It does not immediately defeat the original enterprises, nor does it immediately capture the largest market share. However, it begins to establish its own feedback relationships.

As feedback continues to occur, the new loop gradually acquires more users, data, capabilities, resources, or ecosystem relationships. These newly added stocks further enhance the new loop, endowing it with an increasingly strong self-reinforcing capacity.

Meanwhile, system conditions are also changing. When these changes accumulate to a certain extent, the advantages that originally supported the old loop may no longer hold the same value. The old loop in the existing competitive system does not necessarily disappear suddenly, but the way it accumulates value begins to change.

The New Loop Theory focuses on this process, along with related questions such as:

> _Why do some innovations that initially appear merely as marginal changes gradually form their own growth mechanisms?_
> _Why does having users for a new product not necessarily mean it has established a new competitive logic?_
> _Why might the advantages accumulated by an old loop suddenly lose their original value after system conditions change?_

And the more critical question:

**Under what conditions can a new local loop develop into the new ascendant loop of the system?**

These questions and changes collectively constitute the research object of The New Loop Theory.

# 2. Fundamental Propositions of The Theory

## 2.1 Business System and Competitive System

The **Business System** is the broadest analytical boundary of this theory.

It includes firms, users, suppliers, channels, technologies, capital, institutions, and other interacting participants and resources.

The Business System is not a static environment.

Different participants continuously change each other’s conditions of action. Certain behavioral outcomes further influence future resource flows, user choices, and Value Creation methods.

The **Competitive System** is a specific subsystem or evolutionary stage within the Business System, in which different Value Creation and Value Accumulation Loops compete for resources, users, and dominant Value Accumulation paths.

Therefore, competition is not merely a direct comparison between firms.

At a deeper level, competition is competition between different Value Accumulation Loops.

## 2.2 Old Loop and New Loop

An old loop is the value creation and accumulation mechanism that currently occupies a dominant position in the competitive system. It is usually composed of a series of mutually reinforcing causal relationships and forms a stable resource allocation method through long-term competition.

A new loop, on the other hand, is a value creation and accumulation mechanism that is structurally different from the old loop. It can start from the periphery of the existing competitive system or from a neglected condition within the existing competitive system itself.

A new loop does not initially need to be "larger" than the old loop. What truly matters is that it begins to generate value according to different causal relationships and forms its own accumulation mechanism within these different causal relationships.

Therefore, comparison between New Loop and Old Loop should not first fall on product functions, market size, or individual advantages, Therefore, the comparison between new and old loops should not first fall on product functions or single advantages, but on the level of **how value is created, how feedback is formed, and how it is continuously accumulated**.

## 2.3 The Meaning of System Substitution

The "substitution" mentioned in The New Loop Theory does not mean that old enterprises, old products, or old capabilities must disappear.

An old loop can continue to exist and even retain commercial value for a long time. What truly changes is the way this old loop generates value within the system.

When a new loop continuously changes system conditions, the original stocks of the old loop may still exist, but the value they create is different from the past. Thus, the old loop loses its original reinforcing capacity, while the new loop begins to become a more stable path for value accumulation.

Therefore, substitution primarily manifests as a **change in the dominant path of value accumulation**, rather than a simple change in market share.

## 2.4 Core Definition

The New Loop Theory studies: How new Value Creation and Value Accumulation Loops emerge within existing Business Systems and, through structural Asymmetry, Core Value Accumulation, endogenous Amplification, and changes in System Conditions, eventually become potential dominant Value Accumulation paths within a Competitive System.

Version 1.3.0 further moves this question inside the New Loop itself, examining how the Core Loop forms continuous Value Creation capability around Core Stock.

# 3. Naming the Theory

The Chinese name of the theory is “降维创新.” The English name is The New Loop Theory. These are not literal translations of one another. Each name reflects a different aspect of the same theoretical proposition and is deliberately retained in its respective language.

In it's Chinese name, **"降维 (dimensionality reduction)"** refers to a shift in the level at which an innovation problem is observed. Rather than remaining within the visible dimensions of competition, the analysis moves toward the underlying structures and value relationships that generate those dimensions.

Accordingly, "dimensionality reduction" corresponds to a specific path of observation:

> **Events → Patterns → Structures → Mental Models**

One descends from events to structures, and from structures one seeks new possibilities. Truly significant opportunities for innovation sometimes lie not in the dimension over which current competitors are contending, but in the deeper structures from which that dimension of competition itself emerges.

**The New Loop Theory** takes its English name from the central object of inquiry: the emergence and evolution of new value-accumulation loops within existing competitive systems. “New” does not imply that every element of a New Loop must itself be new. Existing stocks, technologies, capabilities, or resources may be reorganized within a different value relationship and thereby participate in a different accumulation mechanism.

The two names therefore emphasize different dimensions of the theory. “降维创新” emphasizes the shift in perspective required to recognize a different underlying structure; The New Loop Theory emphasizes the new value-accumulation mechanism that may emerge from that shift. Neither name is intended as a literal translation of the other.

This deliberate difference in naming also reflects a concern informed by the history of “Disruptive Innovation.” When a theoretical concept enters a different linguistic and cultural context, its translated name can acquire semantic associations that differ from the concept's original theoretical meaning. Over time, those associations may influence how the concept is understood, applied, and further developed. The distinction between “降维创新” and The New Loop Theory is therefore intentional: it is designed to reduce the risk that the theory's name itself becomes a source of semantic drift.

The New Loop Theory itself is also observed as a reflexive case, where the theory examines its own evolution through the mechanisms it proposes.

# 4. Loop: The Basic Unit of Theoretical Analysis

## 4.1 What is a Loop

In this theory, a loop is not a rhetorical device to describe "circulation."

It refers to a set of causal relationships in which variables influence one another and form feedback loops. When a certain outcome can in turn affect the conditions that produced it, further enhancing or inhibiting the original change, the system has formed a feedback.

Therefore, the key to a loop is not "having many factors," but the existence of a continuous causal closed-loop among these factors.

From this perspective, an enterprise's competitive advantage can be re-understood: advantage is not just the resources an enterprise possesses, but the loop formed among certain resources, behaviors, and outcomes that can continuously reinforce value creation.

It should be noted that a loop does not imply that all feedback relationships within it have the same level of importance. For a specific new loop, it is necessary to further identify which feedback relationships bear the primary role in value accumulation, and which stocks constitute the key carriers of this accumulation.

## 4.2 Reinforcing Loop

Reinforcing loops cause changes in the system to continue expanding in the same direction.

For example, more users bring more feedback, feedback helps improve the product, product improvement enhances user value, thereby attracting more users. What truly matters here is not "user growth," but the mutually reinforcing causal relationship between user growth and product improvement.

Therefore, growth itself cannot prove the existence of a reinforcing loop.

An enterprise can achieve rapid growth through capital investment, price subsidies, or external resources, but if this growth does not form an internal feedback structure that can in turn enhance itself, then this growth does not represent **endogenous Amplification**.

## 4.3 Balancing Loop

Balancing loops counteract changes in the system, driving it towards a certain stable state.

A large number of processes, rules, risk controls, resource allocations, and evaluation mechanisms in mature organizations may serve this function. They help the organization maintain stability and help the system maintain stability over time.

However, balancing loops are not the only source of organizational rigidity. Resource specialization, path dependence, existing interest relationships, organizational structures, and evaluation systems can all further reinforce the old loops.

For a New Loop, even when it possesses clear growth momentum, it may still face constraints from resource consumption, user friction, organizational complexity, competitive responses, and other systemic forces.

Therefore, studying New Loops requires not only identifying Reinforcing Feedback, but also placing these factors back into the overall system for observation.

# 5. From Competitive Advantages to Competitive Loops

## 5.1 Stock

**Stock** is a state or resource accumulated through previous activities within a system, which can influence future system behavior.

Resources, capabilities, user relationships, data, brand trust, channel networks, and other elements within a Business System may all become Stocks.

However, Stocks themselves are not synonymous with competitive advantage. 

The fact that an organization possesses a certain Stock does not mean that this Stock will generate the same value under all System Conditions.

The Value Creation capability of a Stock depends on the Loop it belongs to and how System Conditions influence this Loop.

Therefore, this theory focuses on one fundamental question: **Through what Loop does a certain Stock generate value?**

This question provides an important foundation for understanding Old Loops and New Loops.

This is also a key distinction in The New Loop Theory: **A New Loop does not necessarily require the complete elimination or competitive capture of the stocks already possessed by the Old Loop. What it may change are the System Conditions in which these stocks are situated, and the way these stocks generate value.**

## 5.2 Gain Function

The same Stock may generate completely different value under different System Conditions.

Therefore, the competitive or value return generated by a certain Stock under specific System Conditions can be understood as its **Gain Function**.

It can be expressed as:

**Gain_old(S, C₀) ≠ Gain_old(S, C₁)**

where:

* S represents existing Stock;
* C represents System Conditions.

This means that a resource originally very powerful for an enterprise does not naturally guarantee it will have the same value in the future. One of the important roles of a new loop is precisely to change system conditions, thereby altering the gain functions of the stocks that give the old loop its advantages.

This is a key entry point for understanding "why old advantages become invalid."

When System Conditions change, the Gain Function of existing Stocks may also change. Resources that previously reinforced an Old Loop may no longer possess the same reinforcing capability, while certain Stocks that were previously considered less important may become foundational elements of a New Loop.

## 5.3 How Advantages are Continuously Produced by the System

Traditional competitive analysis often interprets advantage as a certain attribute possessed by an enterprise, such as cost, brand, technology, channel, or scale.

However, from the perspective of a system, these advantages usually do not exist statically.

Scale can bring cost advantages, cost advantages can attract more customers, and more customers can further increase scale; users can generate data, data helps improve products, and improvements attract more users.

> Scale ↑ → Cost ↓ → Price ↓ → Customers ↑ → Scale ↑  
> Users ↑ → Data ↑ → Product Features ↑ → Users ↑

Hidden beneath these causal Loops lies the fundamental reason why New Loops may eventually transform competitive landscapes.

The value of an advantage depends on the system in which it exists.

Therefore, a deep opportunity for innovation comes from redefining "what is valuable" and "why it is valuable."

A Stock that is extremely important within an existing Business System does not necessarily maintain the same Gain Function under new System Conditions. Conversely, a resource previously considered insignificant may become highly valuable under new System Conditions.

## 5.4 Loop vs Loop

If competition is merely comparing resource between enterprises, the core issue of innovation becomes "who has more."

But if competition occurs between loops, the problem changes.

A participant with fewer resources, as long as they can establish a different value creation loop, can avoid directly entering the original competitive dimensions. The early scale of a new loop can even be very small, because what it truly needs to establish is its own feedback structure.

Thus, innovation is no longer just about comparing who has stronger old advantages, but begins to compare: **Which loop can form stronger value accumulation in the future?**

# 6. When Old Loops Become Innovation Constraints

## 6.1 Loop Stabilization

After a loop has operated for a long time, it will produce more and more resources and capabilities that match itself.

The organization begins to recruit, evaluate, invest, and allocate resources according to this loop; customers and channels also gradually adapt to this transaction method.

Thus, the old loop is no longer just a value creation mechanism; it begins to shape the entire organization and market.

## 6.2 Rigidity Effects of Balancing Loops

At this point, the system will tend towards stability under the action of balancing loops, and stable systems need to suppress deviations.

New ideas usually imply uncertainty, resource reallocation, and a decline in short-term metrics; therefore, mature systems will naturally produce a large number of balancing feedback mechanisms.

These mechanisms themselves are not wrong. They are necessary for the organization to maintain stability.

The problem is that when the system needs to explore another loop, the mechanisms originally used to maintain stability may begin to inhibit the formation of the new loop.

## 6.3 Why Successful Systems Struggle to Create Their Own Substitutes

What a successful business system is best at is usually continuing to run the loops it has already validated.

It possesses capabilities, resources, processes, and evaluation standards highly matched with the old loops; therefore, any attempt to deviate from the old loops will face higher internal explanation costs.

This means that success itself may produce a structural constraint: the better the enterprise is at optimizing the current loops, the harder it is to establish a new loop outside the current loops.

This does not mean that mature enterprises cannot innovate, but it illustrates that another loop often needs to gain growth space at the periphery of the existing system or under a different logic of resource allocation.

# 7. The 3A Constraint Framework

The New Loop Theory uses three conditions to describe the basic constraints a new loop faces from its formation to becoming a system force: **Asymmetric, Amplifying, Ascendant**.

They are not three independent labels, but describe three levels of a new loop's evolution from structural formation, dynamic amplification, to system evolution:

- **Asymmetric**: Is it truly a different loop?
- **Amplifying**: Does it have the structural potential to make itself increasingly stronger?
- **Ascendant**: Has this force begun to change the system's dominant value accumulation path?

Therefore, the 3A framework corresponds to theoretical problems at three different levels: **Structure → Dynamics → System Evolution**.

The 3A framework is not a scoring model for predicting success, but a necessary condition framework for identifying whether a new loop possesses the potential for system evolution.

## 7.1 Asymmetric

**Asymmetric** refers to the structural differences between the new loop and the old loop in value creation, revenue formation, or competitive gain functions.

This difference cannot merely come from more functions, lower prices, or better experiences. A product can be excellent yet still operate within the old loop.

True asymmetry needs to answer:

> **If the new loop holds, does it create, obtain, and accumulate value in a different way from the old loop?**

Therefore, being asymmetric is a structural requirement for "newness."

### 7.1.1 Asymmetry is Not Just About Creating New Demand-Side Value

In the process of new loop formation, being asymmetric means the new loop must differ from the old loop in its value creation logic. However, case analysis shows that merely changing demand-side value is insufficient to form a truly new loop.

The business system includes both the demand side and the supply side. The demand side determines how user value is selected, obtained, and evaluated; the supply side determines whether the organization can continuously create, deliver, and accumulate this value in its own way.

If a new loop only changes the demand side, while the supply side still relies on the most expensive and scarce resources in the existing competitive system, it is actually still constrained by the cost structure of the old loop.

Therefore, true asymmetry capable of forming a new loop must ultimately extend to the value relationship between demand and supply.

### 7.1.2 Bilateral Asymmetric Consistency

The structural asymmetry of a new loop does not require simultaneous changes on both the demand and supply sides, but requires the formation of a value coupling relationship between the two that is different from the old loop.

Demand-side Asymmetric answers: **Why do users begin to choose a way of obtaining value that did not exist or was not viable in the past?**

Supply-side Asymmetric answers: **Why can the organization create, deliver, or accumulate this value in a way different from the old loop?**

Together, they determine whether the new loop has truly broken away from the old loop.

At this point, bilateral asymmetric consistency truly answers: **Has a new, mutually supportive relationship been formed between the new demand selection logic and the new supply creation logic?**

If it is only Asymmetric on the demand-side, users gain a new experience, but the enterprise still relies on the old loop's production methods. Such innovation may gain short-term market attention but is easily constrained by the old loop's cost structure.

If it is only Asymmetric on the supply-side, the enterprise possesses a new value creation method, but users cannot perceive the new value. Such innovation also struggles to form an effective market loop.

Therefore, a new loop does not require simultaneous changes in both directions, but ultimately needs to form differences on both the demand and supply sides that can support each other. Only when this new way of obtaining value can form a continuous value exchange and feedback with the supply side can it further enter a new value accumulation loop.

### 7.1.3 Asymmetric Iteration

Being asymmetric is not a one-time assessment completed on the formation of a new loop. For a new loop in the exploration stage, innovators usually need to continuously experiment, adjust, and reconfigure Value Creation relationships within real-world system feedback.

This continuous experimentation and adjustment centered around being structurally asymmetric can be termed **Asymmetric Iteration**.

The object of Asymmetric Iteration is not merely product features, but the relationships among demand, supply, resources, and feedback within the new loop. Innovators can consciously design a new value relationship, or they can discover feedback structures unforeseen from early practices. As system feedback continuously emerges, certain relationships may gradually lose their significance, while others may take on an increasingly important role in value accumulation.

Therefore, Asymmetric Iteration involves both design and discovery. Innovators do not passively wait for a new loop to emerge naturally; rather, they actively shape the potential structure of the new loop through continuous experimentation.

This process also means that the initial design of a new loop is not equivalent to the loop that is ultimately formed. An innovator may initially design a new loop around a certain value proposition, but as user behavior, resource conditions, and system feedback change, the relationships that truly hold long-term value may gradually migrate.

Asymmetric Iteration is therefore not a fixed innovation process, nor is it a fourth condition outside of the 3A framework. Instead, it describes how innovators continuously act within an asymmetric structure, enabling potential new value relationships to gradually form a more stable loop structure through system feedback.

## 7.2 Amplifying

**Amplifying** refers to the existence of a structural self-reinforcing mechanism within the new loop, enabling the operation of the loop to in turn reinforce itself and drive the continuous accumulation of the key stocks it relies on.

Here, it is necessary to particularly distinguish between **Amplifying** nature and **growth**. Growth describes the result, while Amplifying describes the causal structure that produces this result. An existing loop can achieve rapid growth through capital investment, policy support, manual expansion, or market dividends, without thereby possessing self-amplifying capabilities; conversely, a new loop may grow slowly in its early stages, but as long as it has formed a feedback structure within itself that can continuously reinforce itself, it may possess strong self-amplifying potential.

Therefore, the question Amplifying truly needs to answer is not how fast a new loop is growing now, but: **Does this loop have the structural potential to make itself increasingly stronger?**

### 7.2.1 Reinforcing Loops and Self-Amplification

Growth phenomena are easily explained as reinforcing loops. Increases in users, transactions, data, or resources may all present some positive relationship, but these phenomena themselves cannot prove that the new loop has formed a self-amplifying structure.

Growth in a business system may come from continuous external capital input, or from subsidies, manual resource expansion, technological dividends, market cycles, or other one-way driving factors. These factors can all manufacture growth, but they do not necessarily form a feedback loop capable of reinforcing itself.

True self-amplification requires the existence of an endogenous feedback: the results produced by the operation of the loop can in turn reinforce other conditions within the loop, and further improve the loop's ability to continue operating and accumulating. That is to say, the loop does not simply "grow more because it has more resources," but the results produced by its own operation become the conditions for the next round of operation.

This distinction is particularly important for The New Loop Theory. Because if a new loop can only maintain growth by continuously obtaining external resources, then it may possess growth capability, but it has not yet proven that it possesses the structural power to change the competitive system.

### 7.2.2 Net Amplifying

A New Loop not only needs to have a reinforcing loop, but also needs to possess structural amplifying potential sufficient to resist internal resistance within the system. 

The term "net" here does not refer to a value that can already be precisely calculated, but refers to the structural relationship formed between the amplifying mechanism within the new loop and the forces within the system that inhibit its accumulation.

During its operation, a new loop will inevitably be limited by resource consumption, organizational complexity, competitive reactions, user friction, and other balancing mechanisms. At the same time, certain results within it may continuously reinforce key stocks such as users, data, ecosystems, cognition, or others. 

Net Amplifying focuses precisely on this power relationship: whether the amplifying potential generated by the new loop is sufficient to enable it to continuously reinforce itself while being subjected to system resistance.

If a loop's growth must continuously rely on external input to be maintained, then this growth cannot prove it has self-amplifying capabilities; conversely, even if a new loop does not grow significantly in its early stages, as long as it has formed a structure within itself that can continuously reinforce key stocks, it may possess powerful net amplifying potential.

Therefore, "*Net Amplifying ≈ Amplifying Potential - System Resistance*" can be used as an intuitive expression to understand net amplifying, but this expression should not currently be understood as a mathematically defined calculation formula. 

What it intends to express is the theoretical meaning of "net," rather than requiring us to be able to assign precise numerical values to amplifying potential and system resistance right now.

Therefore, net amplifying is closer to a **property of structural potential**, rather than a growth indicator. It describes whether the new loop has internally formed a power structure sufficient to support its own evolution.

### 7.2.3 Reinforcing Loops and Balancing Loops

Reinforcing loops in complex business systems do not exist in isolation. After any new loop begins to operate, it will simultaneously be affected by various limiting factors within the system, and these limiting factors may in turn form balancing loops.

For example, an increase in the number of users may enhance network value and further attract more users to join, thereby forming a reinforcing loop.

> Users increase → Network value increases → More users join

But at the same time, the expansion of user scale may also bring increased service complexity, increased infrastructure pressure, and higher operating costs, thereby forming a balancing loop that inhibits growth.

> Users increase → Service complexity increases → Service costs increase → User experience declines

Therefore, whether a new loop has net amplifying cannot be determined by merely observing whether it has positive feedback, but must simultaneously observe the structural relationship between positive feedback and system balancing forces. What is truly important is not "whether there is a reinforcing loop," but whether the reinforcing loop has sufficient structural power to enable the key stocks relied upon by the new loop to continue accumulating in the presence of these balancing forces.

This is also the most important distinction between net amplifying and ordinary growth. Growth only indicates that stocks are changing, while net amplifying attempts to explain why this change can gradually reinforce the loop that produced it.

### 7.2.4 Case Observation: Bike-Sharing

Bike-sharing presented an obvious reinforcing loop in its early stages. After the increase in vehicle deployment, the coverage area expanded, users gained more usage opportunities, increased orders brought more operational data, and further supported dispatch optimization. This process was able to form obvious scale growth in the early stages.

> More vehicle deployment → Expanded coverage area → Increased user orders → Data accumulation → Dispatch optimization

But at the same time, there were also important balancing forces within the system. The expansion of vehicle scale meant increased vehicle wear and tear, rising maintenance and dispatch costs, and increased offline operational complexity. A larger scale did not mean that each additional vehicle could increase value in the same way; on the contrary, as the system scale expanded, the costs required to maintain this scale might also continuously increase.

> Vehicle scale expands → Vehicle wear and tear increases → Maintenance costs increase → Operational pressure rises  
> Vehicle scale expands → Dispatch complexity increases → Offline operational costs increase

Therefore, what the bike-sharing case illustrates is not that "growth did not occur," but that growth itself is insufficient to prove that a new loop has sufficient net amplifying potential. Only when the positive feedback brought by scale growth can be continuously transformed into key stock accumulation sufficient to offset system resistance can scale truly become the reinforcing mechanism of the new loop itself.

### 7.2.5 Net Amplifying and External Resources

External resources do not naturally mean that a loop lacks self-amplification. External resources such as capital, policies, infrastructure, and market education may all help a new loop cross the early formation stage. The key issue is not whether resources come from inside or outside the system, but whether these resources, after entering, become part of the new loop's own feedback structure.

If external resources can help the new loop establish key stocks such as users, data, technological capabilities, ecosystems, or others, and these stocks can in turn reinforce the new loop, then external resources can become trigger conditions or accelerating factors in the formation process of the new loop.

But if, after the cessation of continuous external input, key stocks immediately stop accumulating, it indicates that the system's growth mainly relies on external driving forces, rather than coming from the loop's own reinforcing mechanism. External resources can trigger, accelerate, or even amplify a new loop, but they cannot replace the new loop's own reinforcing structure.

Therefore, when analyzing a new loop, what truly needs to be judged is not "whether it used external resources," but: **After external resources stop continuous input, does the new loop still possess the structural potential to reinforce itself?

## 7.3 Ascendant

**Ascendant** describes an evolutionary state that a new loop enters at the system level. When a new loop, through continuous development, begins to change system conditions and gradually becomes the system's more primary value accumulation path, it can be said to have entered an Ascendant state.

Therefore, being ascendant is not simply "winning market competition," nor is it a static market share indicator. It describes which value accumulation path in the system is becoming the main direction, and whether resources, users, and other key stocks are beginning to reorganize around this new path.

### 7.3.1 Ascendancy is Not a Static Result

A new loop becoming dominant does not mean it gains a permanent advantage. It means that under specific system states, this loop has become the main direction of value accumulation.

As new loops appear, the system may change again. Therefore, ascendancy needs to be understood as a dynamic state, rather than a final result. A loop can become the system's primary attractor in a certain stage, and subsequently lose this status due to changes in system conditions.

This means that ascendancy needs to be understood on two scales simultaneously. One scale focuses on whether the new loop has become the dominant value accumulation path in the current stage; the other scale focuses on whether this dominant path itself will migrate with system evolution. The former belongs to Ascendant, while the latter enters the long-term evolution process described by Sequential Ascendance.

### 7.3.2 Sequential Ascendance

Sequential ascendance does not negate ascendancy, but places ascendancy back into the complex business system for understanding. Dominance is not the endpoint, but the starting point for the next round of system evolution.

The business system is not a static environment. The formation of every new loop will change the behavior of participants, the direction of resource flow, and the path of value accumulation, and these changes will create new opportunity spaces, enabling new loops to form around the changed system conditions.

Therefore, **Sequential Ascendance** describes: **In a complex business system, the dominant value accumulation path continuously migrates with changes in system conditions. This migration may be realized through competition, substitution, or reconstruction between the new value loop and the original dominant loop.**

It needs to be specially noted here that Sequential Ascendance does not describe the continuous victories and defeats between enterprises or products, but the continuous migration of the system's dominant value accumulation path. A new dominant loop does not necessarily need to simply defeat the old loop, but may also gradually cause the original value accumulation path to lose its dominant position by recombining resources, changing value relationships, or reconstructing competitive conditions.

### 7.3.3 Attractor

Complex systems do not develop in all directions limitlessly. After long-term feedback, the system tends to move towards certain relatively stable states or paths, which can be understood using the concept of attractors.

In a business system, an attractor can be understood as the dominant value accumulation path that the system occupies over the long term.

Therefore, the true **Ascendant** state of a new loop is not simply "becoming number one in the market," but rather it beginning to become the system's more primary and stable value accumulation path.

This is why the ultimate change The New Loop Theory focuses on is not the sales volume of a certain product, but where the system itself is converging.

### 7.3.4 From Substitution Relationship to Attractor Competition

Traditional innovation narratives often adopt a linear substitution model: old technology has problems, new technology appears, and new technology ultimately replaces old technology. This model is applicable to some technology substitution scenarios, but is insufficient to explain long-term evolution in complex business systems.

In complex adaptive systems, a new loop does not just defeat the old loop, but changes what resources are worth accumulating and which feedback relationships can continuously generate value. The old loop can continue to exist, but gradually loses the concentration of system resources and user attention; the new loop changes the resource flow in the system through a new value accumulation method.

Therefore, the evolution of the business system is closer to a continuous process of the old loop forming dominance, the new loop appearing, competition between different value accumulation methods, changes in system conditions, and the new loop becoming the new attractor. 

Why a once successful new loop will eventually lose its dominance is usually not because it suddenly loses value, but because system conditions have changed, making its original gain function no longer have the same effect.

The gain function of a new loop depends on its relationship with the system conditions at that time. When key conditions such as user behavior, technological foundation, cost structure, and industrial connection methods change, the advantages in the original loop may decrease, or even transform into new limitations.

What truly migrates is what is worth being continuously accumulated in the system, and what feedback relationships can continue to form value.

## 7.4 The Causal Relationship within The 3A Framework

The three conditions of 3A can be understood within a basic dynamic relationship. 

Being **Asymmetric** first changes the structure of the new loop, making it no longer just a local optimization of the old loop; being **Amplifying** further determines whether this structural difference can be continuously reinforced through endogenous feedback and drive the accumulation of key stocks; when key stocks reach a certain level, the new loop may begin to influence system conditions and further change the dominant value accumulation path in the system, becoming **Ascendant**.

Therefore, the 3A framework describes a directional causal relationship, but there is no transmission of sufficiency among the 3A conditions: the satisfaction of one condition does not constitute a reason for the next condition to hold.

The arrows here describe a theoretical dynamic relationship, not a deterministic predictive relationship. Structural adjustments of a new loop, the formation of amplifying dynamics, and system feedback can occur repeatedly throughout the evolutionary process.

The 3A framework remains a necessary-condition framework, not a success-prediction model. # 8. Core Mechanisms

The 3A Framework introduced in the previous chapter addresses the structural and dynamic conditions under which a New Loop may gradually acquire system-level ascendance.

Yet asymmetry, amplification, and ascendance alone do not explain a more specific question: **through what structure does a New Loop accumulate strength, and how does it develop an internal basis capable of continuously reinforcing itself within an existing Business System?**

This involves three related but theoretically distinct mechanisms: **Core Loop**, **Core Stock**, and **Stock Migration**.

* **Core Loop** describes the most consequential value-creation and accumulation relationship within a New Loop;
* **Core Stock** describes the structural state accumulated through that relationship and capable of reinforcing the loop in return;
* **Stock Migration** describes how existing stocks within a Business System are reorganized under a new value-creation logic and incorporated into a new value cycle.

Together, these mechanisms explain how a New Loop develops an internal basis for sustained evolution. They do not, however, constitute a fixed sequence. A Core Loop may be deliberately designed or gradually stabilized through experimentation and iteration. Core Stock may be identified, adjusted, or migrated during the process. Stock Migration may occur before, during, or after the formation of a New Loop.

![mechanism mapping](assets/image/mmai_en.svg)

The purpose of this chapter is therefore not to define an innovation process, but to examine several structural mechanisms that are consequential to the formation and evolution of a New Loop.

## 8.1 Core Loop

A New Loop typically contains multiple value relationships, but some relationships play a more fundamental role in value creation and value accumulation than others. They determine why the New Loop can continue to operate and why certain outcomes generated by the loop can in turn reinforce subsequent value creation.

This set of critical causal relationships is referred to as the **Core Loop**.

Core Loop does not imply that a New Loop contains only one cycle, nor that its core structure must be fully identified before innovation begins. In some cases, the Core Loop may emerge from the deliberate design of a distinctive business model: an innovator identifies an underutilized value-creation capability and designs feedback relationships around it. In other cases, the Core Loop may gradually emerge through continued experimentation.

Therefore, Core Loop has both **designed** and **emergent** characteristics.

An innovator may deliberately construct a set of asymmetric value relationships and use experimentation to determine whether they can generate sustained feedback. As iteration proceeds, the relative importance of different feedback relationships may change. Some may gradually assume a greater role in value accumulation and eventually become the relatively stable Core Loop of the New Loop.

This process is directly related to **Asymmetric Iteration**, discussed in Chapter 7. Asymmetric Iteration is not a fixed set of innovation steps, but a mechanism for continuously adjusting value relationships. Innovators continually modify, combine, and validate different value relationships, allowing those with stronger potential for self-amplification to gradually assume a more important position.

A Core Loop initially designed by an innovator therefore does not necessarily remain the final core of the New Loop. As System Conditions change, user feedback accumulates, and different stocks develop, the original design may be reinforced, reconfigured, or eventually replaced by another set of value relationships.

This is why The New Loop Theory does not treat innovation as a one-time act of discovery. A New Loop may be deliberately designed, but what that loop ultimately becomes must be shaped through continued feedback and selection within the real system.

Structurally, the Core Loop connects value creation with value accumulation. Its outputs, if they remain one-time results, do not constitute genuine self-reinforcement. Only when those outputs can be converted into states that accumulate over time and reduce the resistance to subsequent value creation and accumulation can the Core Loop gradually develop sustained reinforcing potential.

The Core Loop therefore ultimately raises a fundamental question:

**What, exactly, is this loop accumulating over time?**

That question leads naturally to Core Stock.

## 8.2 Core Stock

Chapter 7 introduced the concept of **Key Stock**. Key Stock is used to identify a concrete object of accumulation when examining the self-reinforcing mechanisms of a New Loop. It may take the form of a user network, data, technical capability, a developer ecosystem, brand trust, a distribution network, organizational capability, or a more specific business indicator.

Core Stock is related to Key Stock, but the two are not equivalent.

Key Stock asks: **What is this loop accumulating, and can that accumulation reinforce the loop in return?**

Core Stock asks a further question: **What accumulated state embodies the distinctive value-creation capability of this New Loop and constitutes the basis of its core self-reinforcing potential?**

Core Stock is therefore not simply the “most important resource,” nor is it necessarily the largest asset held by a firm. Its theoretical significance lies in the fact that it carries the distinctive value-creation capability of the New Loop and can be accumulated through the Core Loop, eventually becoming an important basis for sustained self-reinforcement.

In this sense, Core Stock does not necessarily take the form of a single, discrete, directly measurable indicator. It may exist as a **structural state** composed of multiple interrelated stocks and states. These states are not merely aggregated side by side. They are connected through the Core Loop in mutually reinforcing relationships and collectively represent the accumulated value-creation capability of the New Loop.

Core Stock can therefore be understood as a **Structural Stock**: it describes not simply how much of an isolated object has accumulated, but what kind of accumulated state a New Loop has developed through continued operation that enables and reinforces future value creation.

### 8.2.1 Structural Stock

Traditional business analysis tends to treat stock as an independently identifiable and measurable object: the number of users, the volume of data, the quantity of content, distribution coverage, installed base, number of developers, or accumulated brand assets. Such measures may constitute important stocks, but they do not thereby become Core Stock.

Core Stock concerns the structural relationships among such stocks and whether those relationships have become part of the basis through which the Core Loop continues to generate value.

A Core Stock may therefore consist of multiple interrelated states. Any one state may be insufficient to explain the New Loop's continuing value-creation capability, while the relationships among these states may together constitute the Core Stock.

This structural character does not make Core Stock unobservable. It means that observing it cannot be reduced to identifying a single definitive metric.

For example, a New Loop may simultaneously accumulate user participation, behavioral data, product understanding, content capability, and distribution capability. Each individual metric provides only a partial view of the structure. What determines the New Loop's future value-generation capability may instead be the relationships that have formed among these states.

The relevant question is therefore not:

> “Which metric is growing fastest?”

but:

> **“Which accumulated states are jointly changing the future Gain Function of the loop?”**

This distinction also means that Core Stock is dynamic. As a New Loop enters different stages of development, its structural configuration may migrate, be reconfigured, or be redefined. A stock relationship that is central at one stage may no longer perform the same value-creation function after System Conditions change, while another set of states may gradually assume a more central role.

Core Stock is therefore not a permanently fixed “core asset” owned by a firm. It is a structural state that evolves together with the Core Loop.

The relationship between Core Loop and Core Stock can consequently be understood as mutually reinforcing:

**The Core Loop continuously creates and accumulates Core Stock, while Core Stock in turn reinforces the Core Loop.**

When this relationship becomes sufficiently stable and generates Net Amplifying effects, Core Stock becomes an important internal source of sustained system strength within the New Loop.

This also means that observed growth alone is insufficient evidence that Core Stock has formed. Growth in users, revenue, installed base, or other outcome variables may result from external resource inputs, capital investment, or other temporary conditions. Such outcomes may accompany the formation of Core Stock, but they cannot establish it by themselves.

Conversely, Core Stock may begin to form before a New Loop demonstrates superior performance on conventional outcome measures. A New Loop may operate with external support while gradually accumulating structural states capable of reinforcing itself, even when its financial or market outcomes remain weak.

Core Stock must therefore be evaluated through the loop itself: **is a structural state accumulating through the operation of the Core Loop, and is that state continuously changing the future value-generation capability of the loop?**

Core Stock thus serves as a bridge between Core Loop and self-reinforcement.

### 8.2.2 Key Differences from Traditional Business Analysis Language

The most important difference between Core Stock and traditional business-analysis language does not lie in the objects being observed, but in the **analytical level at which those objects are examined**.

Traditional business analysis often begins with outcome variables or resource variables: how many users a company has, how much data it holds, how many developers participate, how many stores it operates, how large its installed base is, or how much revenue it generates. These measures describe the current state of a system, but they do not necessarily explain why that state can continue to generate future value.

The New Loop Theory asks a further set of questions: through what loop do these stocks generate value? Have they formed mutually reinforcing relationships? Are those relationships changing the future Gain Function?

Core Stock should therefore not be equated with:

**the largest stock, the fastest-growing metric, the most expensive asset, or the business variable that is easiest to measure.**

A stock may be enormous and still remain merely a general stock if it is inherited from an Old Loop and has not entered the new value-creation structure.

Conversely, a relatively small or financially insignificant accumulation may already constitute part of Core Stock if it has entered the Core Loop and begun to change future value-generation capability.

This distinction also explains why **external support does not, by itself, disprove self-reinforcement**.

External resources may sustain a New Loop long enough for it to accumulate the structural states required for further reinforcement. But external input is not itself Core Stock. The relevant question is whether those inputs are being transformed, through the operation of the loop, into internal accumulation capable of reinforcing the Core Loop.

Thus:

> **External support may sustain a loop, but it cannot substitute for self-reinforcement.**

Likewise:

> **Observed growth is neither sufficient evidence of self-reinforcement nor necessary evidence of Core Stock formation.**

This distinction is fundamental to the difference between Core Stock and outcome-oriented business analysis.

Core Stock is not primarily concerned with **how much value has already been realized**. It is concerned with whether the New Loop is accumulating a structural state capable of **continuously increasing its future value-generation capability**.

This also clarifies the relationship between Core Stock and Key Stock.

* **Key Stock** refers to a more concrete and observable stock. It can serve as an analytical unit for identifying accumulation within a New Loop, and it may constitute a component of, or an observable proxy for, Core Stock.

* **Core Stock**, by contrast, is a higher-level structural concept. It may consist of a single Key Stock, but it may also consist of multiple interrelated Key Stocks and the relationships among them. No individual Key Stock necessarily represents the Core Stock as a whole.

Core Stock is therefore not the simple sum of several Key Stocks. It describes the structural state formed by those stocks within the Core Loop and their capacity to jointly sustain future value generation.

This distinction allows Core Stock to remain a structural concept within the theory while preserving Key Stock as a more concrete and analytically observable term.

Core Stock also does not necessarily originate through Stock Migration. It may emerge gradually through the operation of a New Loop, or it may be deliberately cultivated around a new value-creation capability.

This leads to the distinct mechanism of Stock Migration.

## 8.3 Stock Migration

In a complex Business System, the formation of a New Loop does not necessarily require the construction of an entirely independent value-creation system from the ground up. Mature Business Systems accumulate substantial stocks over time: user relationships, technical capabilities, brand trust, supply networks, transaction data, organizational experience, and other forms of accumulated capacity. These stocks initially serve an Old Loop, and their value is consequently shaped by the Gain Function of that loop.

When a new form of value creation emerges, some of these stocks may become detached from their original cycle and acquire a different function within a new value-creation logic.

This phenomenon is referred to as **Stock Migration**.

Stock Migration is the process through which existing stocks within a Business System are reorganized under a new value-creation logic and incorporated into a new value cycle.

The essence of Stock Migration is therefore not the movement of a resource from one business unit to another, nor the simple replication of resources from an existing business in a new business. What migrates is the **role that the stock plays within the value-creation structure**.

The same resource can generate entirely different value within different loops. A platform with a large user base, for example, may use that user stock for advertising distribution, transaction matching, content recommendation, or ecosystem development. The user stock itself does not determine its ultimate value. What matters is the feedback structure into which those users are connected.

Stock Migration can therefore be expressed as: 

**Existing Stock → Reorganization → New Value Cycle**

Only when an existing stock enters a new cycle and changes the way value is accumulated does genuine Stock Migration occur.

### 8.3.1 From Resource Inheritance to Value Reconstruction

This also explains why a firm may possess substantial historical accumulation and still fail to establish a new growth cycle. Resources do not automatically generate a new form of value. If a migrated resource continues to operate according to the logic of the Old Loop, it merely extends an existing capability rather than creating a new accumulation logic.

Genuine Stock Migration requires the reorganized stock to serve a new direction of value accumulation and, through that reorganization, to change its role within the Competitive System.

From the perspective of New Loop formation, Stock Migration provides an important path. An innovator does not necessarily need to compete directly with the dominant loop across every dimension. Instead, the innovator may identify stocks within the existing Business System whose value has not been fully realized under the current value logic and redefine their role within a different value-creation structure.

This can materially change the conditions under which a New Loop forms.

A New Loop built entirely from scratch must establish its value logic, user relationships, resource accumulation, and market recognition simultaneously. Stock Migration allows an innovator to draw on part of the accumulation already present in the existing system and reconnect those resources to a new value-creation structure, thereby reducing some of the costs of establishing the New Loop.

Stock Migration, however, does not imply that a new business inheriting resources from an Old Loop will necessarily become a New Loop. The critical question remains whether those stocks have entered a new feedback structure and begun to serve a different direction of value accumulation.

The theoretical object of Stock Migration is therefore the broader category of **Stock**, rather than **Core Stock**. A stock that enters a New Loop through migration may eventually become part of Core Stock, but it may also remain merely a supporting resource that helps establish the early conditions of the New Loop.

Core Loop, Core Stock, and Stock Migration therefore do not form a fixed sequence. A Core Loop may be designed before it stabilizes through iteration. Core Stock may be deliberately identified or gradually revealed through feedback. Stock Migration may provide an early foundation for a New Loop or continue after the New Loop has already formed.

The three mechanisms perform different theoretical functions:

* **Core Loop** describes the most consequential value-creation and accumulation relationship within a New Loop;

* **Core Stock** describes the structural state accumulated through that relationship and capable of reinforcing the New Loop in return;

* **Stock Migration** describes how existing stocks within a Business System are reorganized and incorporated into a new value cycle.

Their relationship is therefore not a linear innovation process, but an evolving system process.

# 9. Unified Explanatory Framework for Case Observations

> **Nature of Case Observations:** The cases in this chapter are illustrative observations of theoretical mechanisms intended to make the abstract concepts of The New Loop Theory more concrete. Case details are compiled from publicly available business histories and industry materials and should not be regarded as rigorous historical or empirical academic research.

The preceding chapters established the core mechanisms of The New Loop Theory: value accumulation within a competitive system is not a static structure, but continuously migrates between old loops and new loops; new loops establish value-creation relationships distinct from those of old loops through asymmetric structures, develop self-amplifying capabilities through the support of Core Loop and Core Stock, and may ultimately become new dominant paths of value accumulation.

The purpose of case observation is not to force every case into a predefined set of theoretical concepts, but to re-examine a specific competitive system: How was value originally created and accumulated? What structural changes occurred? Which stocks were reorganized? How did new value-creation relationships emerge and acquire self-amplifying capabilities? When system conditions changed further, how did existing stocks migrate, become revalued, or lose their previous functions?

Therefore, this chapter does not assume that every new loop must pass through all mechanisms in the same sequence. **Asymmetric Structure, Asymmetric Iteration, Core Loop, Core Stock, self-amplification, and Stock Migration may occur simultaneously and interact with one another within the same evolutionary process.** The purpose of case observation is to identify the actual relationships among these mechanisms across different competitive systems, rather than to search for a fixed path applicable to all innovations.

## 9.1 Netflix

### 9.1.1 The Old Loop: Value Accumulation Through Physical Rental

Before Netflix entered the market, the primary path of value accumulation in household video consumption was represented by physical rental chains such as Blockbuster. Its basic loop can be summarized as:

**Store Coverage → Physical Inventory → In-Store Selection → Per-Transaction Rental → Rental Revenue → Store Expansion and Inventory Renewal**

Within this structure, the store network and physical inventory were important sources of value. The more stores there were, the broader the coverage and the greater the convenience available to users. More user transactions, in turn, provided the revenue base for store expansion and inventory renewal. Store scale, inventory scale, and user coverage therefore formed mutually supportive relationships.

However, this loop was also constrained by the physical operating structure itself. Store expansion meant that rent, labor, inventory allocation, logistics, and other costs increased simultaneously. The physical network was both an important stock through which the old loop created value and a structural condition that constrained its further expansion.

Therefore, the problem faced by Blockbuster cannot simply be understood as “insufficient store efficiency” or “insufficiently good online service.” The more important question was: **As content consumption gradually detached from physical space, did the store network as a Core Stock still possess the same gain function?**

### 9.1.2 The New Loop: From One-Time Transactions to an Ongoing Relationship

Netflix initially adopted a DVD-by-mail rental model. Viewed purely as a product format, mailing DVDs might appear to be nothing more than changing “picking up a movie at a store” into “having a movie mailed to one's home.” From the perspective of value-creation relationships, however, it had already begun to change the old loop.

Blockbuster's transactional relationship was built around individual rental transactions. Users repeatedly needed to visit stores, select films, and pay rental fees. Netflix, by contrast, established an ongoing relationship through monthly subscriptions and eliminated traditional late fees. Users no longer needed to organize their viewing behavior around individual transactions, but could freely select and return films within an ongoing subscription relationship.

Therefore, Netflix's initial asymmetry did not come from “mailing” itself, but from **reorganizing the relationship between how users obtained value and how the enterprise captured value**. Users moved from “in-store selection and per-transaction rental” toward “continuous subscription and at-home selection,” while the enterprise shifted from dependence on store transactions toward dependence on continuous user relationships.

This change was subsequently amplified by streaming. After 2007, Netflix gradually transitioned from DVD rental to online streaming. Physical media and delivery were further removed, and content distribution began to occur directly through digital networks. Netflix therefore did not simply move its existing business “online.” Instead, it continuously adjusted its value-creation structure, gradually connecting user relationships, content distribution, viewing behavior, and data feedback within the same value cycle.

This process demonstrates the role of **Asymmetric Iteration**: the new loop did not exist in complete form when Netflix first entered the market. DVD subscriptions, streaming, personalized recommendation, and content investment were the results of Netflix continuously reconfiguring value relationships under changing system conditions.

### 9.1.3 Core Loop: From User Behavior to Content Value

As its user base and viewing behavior accumulated, Netflix gradually developed a set of interconnected feedback relationships:

**User Viewing → Behavioral Feedback → Content Understanding and Recommendation → Content Discovery → Viewing and Retention → More Behavioral Feedback**

The recommendation system was an important amplification mechanism, but it could not by itself represent the entire Core Loop of Netflix. As Netflix expanded further into original content, user behavioral data also began to influence content selection and investment, extending the value cycle from “how to distribute existing content” toward “how to decide what content to produce.”

A more complete core value-creation relationship gradually emerged:

**User Relationship → Behavioral Feedback → Content Understanding → Content Selection and Investment → Content Supply → User Value → User Relationship**

The important change in this structure was that user behavior was no longer merely an outcome left behind after a transaction occurred. It became an input into the next round of value creation. The more users Netflix had and the richer their viewing behavior became, the more effectively Netflix could understand the content preferences of different user groups. This understanding could then influence recommendations, content acquisition, and original content investment. Content that better matched user needs further strengthened user relationships and generated new behavioral feedback.

Therefore, Netflix's self-amplifying capability was not simply “more users means more revenue.” The theoretically significant mechanism was that **the stock generated by one value-creation stage was reinvested into the next round of value creation, giving the entire loop an endogenous capacity for reinforcement.**

### 9.1.4 Core Stock: A Structural Stock Composed of Multiple Stock Relationships

Viewed through this Core Loop, Netflix's Core Stock is difficult to reduce to a single indicator.

User numbers can increase, data volume can increase, and recommendation accuracy can improve, but increases in these indicators individually do not necessarily produce the value-creation capability that Netflix requires. What is truly important is the structural relationship formed among long-term user relationships, user behavioral data, content understanding capabilities, recommendation capabilities, and content selection and investment capabilities.

Therefore, Netflix's Core Stock is better understood as a **Structural Stock**: a structure of interconnected stocks that jointly carries its core value-creation capability.

This also explains why “user data” alone is insufficient as a complete explanation. Competitors can also obtain user data. What is difficult to replicate in a short period is the combination of long-term user relationships and accumulated behavioral data, together with the overall structure formed when this data is transformed through recommendation systems, content understanding, and content investment capabilities.

In other words, Netflix's Core Stock is not “how much data it possesses,” but **the extent to which it has developed a structural capability to continuously transform user behavior into content value, user value, and the next round of behavioral feedback.**

### 9.1.5 Stock Migration: Reorganizing Stocks Within the New Loop

Netflix's development process is particularly useful for demonstrating that Stock Migration does not simply mean “moving a stock from A to B.”

The first important migration occurred during the transition from DVD rental to streaming. The user relationships, brand trust, and large amounts of user behavioral information that Netflix accumulated during its DVD business did not lose their value as the medium changed. These stocks were reorganized into the streaming business, allowing existing user relationships to continue serving as the foundation of the new loop.

A second important migration occurred as Netflix extended from content distribution into content production. The data initially accumulated by Netflix was primarily used to understand users and improve content distribution. As capabilities continued to accumulate, this information began to enter content acquisition and original-content investment. User behavioral data thus shifted from being feedback information within the distribution process to becoming an input into content-production decisions.

*House of Cards* is often used to illustrate this change. Its theoretical significance is not merely that “Netflix used data to produce a successful television series,” but that **stocks originally belonging to the consumption and distribution stages were reconfigured into the content-production stage, thereby changing the way content value was created.**

This means that Stock Migration is not simply the transfer of an old stock from one business function to another. Rather, the stock acquires a new role and a new gain function within a new value-creation relationship.

### 9.1.6 Interaction Among the Core Mechanisms

Netflix's evolutionary process cannot be compressed into a linear chain of “Asymmetry → Core Loop → Core Stock → Self-Amplification → Ascendance.”

The asymmetric structure initially changed the user relationship and the way value was obtained. Asymmetric Iteration then continuously adjusted this structure, gradually connecting DVD subscriptions, streaming, recommendation, and content production. During this process, user relationships, behavioral data, content understanding, and content-production capabilities gradually formed a Structural Stock, which in turn reinforced the Core Loop.

Netflix's case is therefore better understood as **a process in which a new loop gradually forms, continuously iterates, and expands the scope of value creation through Stock Migration**.

As an increasing amount of video consumption detached from physical stores, the systemic value of the original store network declined, while the gain function of Netflix's accumulated user relationships, data, and digital content capabilities increased. What occurred was not simply that one company “performed better” than another. Rather, the dominant path of value accumulation within the competitive system changed.

Netflix therefore provides an important case observation: **a new loop does not necessarily exist in complete form at the beginning. A new value-creation relationship can begin with a local asymmetric structure, continuously iterate through real feedback, and gradually form a new system attractor through Stock Migration and self-amplification.**

## 9.2 NVIDIA and CUDA

### 9.2.1 The Old Loop: GPU as a Graphics Computing Resource

Before CUDA emerged, the primary value of GPUs came from graphics rendering. NVIDIA's traditional value-accumulation relationship can be summarized as:

**Gaming and Graphics Demand → GPU Architecture Development → Graphics Card Sales → Revenue Reinvestment → Next-Generation GPU Development**

GPU architecture capabilities, chip development capabilities, the installed base of graphics cards, and developer relationships formed around graphics applications jointly supported this loop.

The key limitation of this loop was not that GPUs lacked computing power, but that this computing power was primarily encapsulated within graphics APIs and graphics application frameworks. Large amounts of GPU computing resources were therefore constrained to specific value-creation contexts.

From within the old loop, improving GPU graphics performance remained a reasonable optimization direction. But from the perspective of the broader computing system, a question began to emerge:

**If the large-scale parallel computing capability of GPUs could be directly accessed by a much broader range of software developers, could its path of value accumulation change?**

CUDA emerged around this question.

### 9.2.2 The New Loop: From Graphics Processor to Computing Platform

In 2006, NVIDIA introduced CUDA, enabling developers to use relatively familiar programming approaches to invoke GPUs for general-purpose parallel computing.

The asymmetry of CUDA was not simply that “GPU computing was faster.” More importantly, it changed the definition of the GPU's value. The GPU was no longer only a graphics-rendering device to be purchased and used. It began to function as a general-purpose accelerated computing platform for scientific computing, engineering computing, and eventually artificial intelligence computing.

This changed the demand side. The potential users of GPUs were no longer limited to gamers and graphics application developers, but expanded to scientists, engineers, researchers, and a much broader range of software developers.

The supply side also changed. NVIDIA did not achieve this transition by building a completely independent new hardware system. Instead, it continuously integrated CUDA computing capabilities into its GPU products and software stack, allowing the large installed base of existing GPUs to become the foundation of a new computing platform.

This created a two-sided asymmetry with important theoretical significance:

**Demand side: from graphics demand to general-purpose parallel computing demand;**

**Supply side: from the sale of standalone GPU hardware to a computing platform composed jointly of hardware, software, development tools, and application ecosystems.**

CUDA therefore changed not merely a product feature, but the value relationship between GPUs and developers.

### 9.2.3 Core Loop: Mutual Reinforcement Between the Installed Base and Developer Ecosystem

The core reinforcing relationship gradually formed by CUDA can be summarized as:

**GPU Installed Base Expands → Developer Base Expands → Tools and Application Ecosystem Becomes Richer → Platform Value Increases → More Computing Demand Adopts CUDA GPUs → GPU Installed Base Expands Further**

What is truly important here is not the general commercial logic of “more developers means more revenue,” but that **hardware stock and software ecosystem formed a mutually reinforcing cross-layer loop**.

A larger installed base of GPUs lowered the opportunity cost for developers to enter the platform. More developers, in turn, drove the continued growth of libraries, tools, and applications. As the software ecosystem became richer, CUDA GPUs could solve a broader range of problems. More application scenarios further increased the value of GPUs as a computing platform.

AlexNet in 2012 was an important observation point in the formation of this structure. Deep-learning researchers used GPUs to train large-scale neural networks, bringing GPU computing capabilities originally used primarily for graphics computing into a new application domain. More importantly, the development tools, developer relationships, and GPU installed base that CUDA had accumulated beforehand enabled this new application domain to build relatively quickly on the existing platform.

Therefore, deep learning was not simply an “external cause” of CUDA's self-amplifying mechanism. It was more like a new opportunity created by a change in system conditions, while the stocks that CUDA had already accumulated determined whether this opportunity could be rapidly absorbed by the new loop.

This also demonstrates the **distinction between external opportunity and self-amplification**: external change can trigger, accelerate, or even redirect the development of a new loop, but only when existing stocks have already formed a self-reinforcing structure can an external opportunity be transformed into sustained endogenous growth.

### 9.2.4 Core Stock: Structural Stock Within the CUDA Loop

CUDA's Core Stock likewise cannot be reduced to a single number.

Developer count, GPU installed base, software-library count, application count, and download volume can all serve as observable indicators, but none of them individually explains why CUDA can continuously generate platform value.

What truly matters is the structural relationship among these stocks:

**The installed base provides developers with the hardware foundation; developer investment produces tools and applications; tools and applications increase platform utility; backward compatibility reduces the erosion of existing development investment across generations; and long-term software investment and organizational commitment further increase developers' willingness to remain on the platform.**

Therefore, CUDA's Core Stock is better understood as a **Structural Stock** jointly composed of the developer ecosystem, GPU installed base, software toolchain, application support, backward compatibility, and long-term developer investment.

No individual indicator can represent this stock by itself.

For example, a large GPU installed base does not automatically produce the CUDA ecosystem; nor does a large developer base necessarily mean that complete platform value exists. What possesses amplifying power is the interdependence among these stocks and the value-creation capability they collectively carry.

This also explains the difference between CUDA and a simple software tool. A software tool can be copied, and an API can be redesigned, but a structural stock composed through long-term accumulation of a hardware base, developer relationships, toolchains, application ecosystems, and developer investment has entirely different migration costs and structural inertia.

### 9.2.5 Stock Migration: Reconfiguring the Value of Existing GPU Stocks

Another important theoretical significance of CUDA is that it demonstrates how Stock Migration can change the value of existing assets.

During the era of graphics computing, NVIDIA accumulated substantial GPU architecture capabilities, a hardware installed base, and developer relationships. These stocks initially served graphics rendering. CUDA did not build a new loop independently of these stocks. Instead, it enabled them to enter a new value-creation relationship.

When new computing demands such as scientific computing and deep learning emerged, the GPU installed base originally serving graphics computing began to perform new functions. Existing stocks consequently began to acquire new value within the new loop.

This process demonstrates that Stock Migration is not equivalent to moving an asset from an old business into a new business. The deeper change is:

**The role of the same stock changes within a different value-creation structure.**

GPU capabilities that originally determined “how complex an image could be rendered” began to help determine “how quickly parallel computations could be completed.” The developer and installed-base relationships originally formed around gaming began to support scientific computing and artificial intelligence applications.

As system conditions continued to change, this migration could continue. The software and hardware foundation formed around CUDA gradually expanded from AI training into inference and broader accelerated-computing tasks. Stock Migration is therefore not necessarily a one-time event, but may continue as system conditions evolve.

### 9.2.6 Interaction Among the Core Mechanisms

The CUDA case likewise cannot be understood as an innovation process completed through a fixed sequence.

NVIDIA first established an asymmetric relationship between GPUs and general-purpose computing demand through CUDA, while continuously investing in development tools, software ecosystems, and developer resources around this relationship. As the installed base and developer ecosystem accumulated, the Core Loop gradually formed; the Core Loop then continued to expand and reinforce the Core Stock.

At the same time, the existing GPU installed base underwent Stock Migration: stocks originally belonging to the graphics-computing loop began to enter general-purpose computing and AI-computing loops. External application opportunities, particularly the development of deep learning, enabled these accumulated stocks to acquire new value gain functions and further expand the ecosystem.

The CUDA case is therefore better understood through the following mechanism:

**Asymmetric Structure determines how existing resources can be reorganized into new value relationships; Core Loop enables these relationships to self-reinforce; Core Stock determines whether this self-reinforcement can accumulate sufficient structural potential; Stock Migration enables stocks from the existing system to enter a new path of value accumulation.**

When these mechanisms operate together, a new path of value accumulation may gradually acquire system-level ascendance.

### 9.2.7 Continuous Ascendance: A New Dominant Path Can Still Be Reorganized

The ecosystem advantage formed by CUDA today does not mean that this path of value accumulation has become permanently stable.

New computing architectures, software ecosystems, development methods, and AI application models may all change the gain functions of different computing platforms. As system conditions evolve, stocks that possess high value today may be revalued, while new loops may begin to form at the edges of existing structures.

Therefore, ascendance is not a state in which the process ends once a company has “won the market.” After a new loop becomes the dominant path of value accumulation, its Core Stock remains subject to continuous evolution; meanwhile, other loops may continue accumulating their own Core Stocks and wait for changes in system conditions.

The CUDA case therefore further illustrates the meaning of **Continuous Ascendance**: ascendance within a competitive system is not a one-time victory between enterprises, but an ongoing process in which the dominant path of value accumulation continues to migrate as system conditions change.

## 9.3 Unified Observation of the Two Cases

Netflix and NVIDIA operate in entirely different businesses. The former changed the relationships among the distribution, consumption, and production of video content; the latter changed the relationship between computing resources and software development. From the perspective of The New Loop Theory, however, both can be understood as **reorganizing value-creation and value-accumulation relationships within an existing competitive system**.

Netflix did not build a completely separate new loop from the existing video industry. Beginning with DVD subscriptions, it reorganized user relationships, content distribution, and behavioral feedback, and through continuous iteration further migrated these stocks into streaming, recommendation, and content production. Its new loop therefore gradually moved from the edge of the existing competitive system toward its center.

CUDA likewise did not exist independently of NVIDIA's existing GPU capabilities. It redefined the boundaries of GPU use, allowing the installed base, hardware capabilities, and developer relationships originally serving graphics computing to enter new value cycles in general-purpose computing and artificial intelligence. As the software ecosystem and hardware base mutually reinforced one another, these existing stocks acquired new value gain functions.

The two cases therefore jointly reveal an important distinction:

**The formation of a new loop does not mean that everything within the old loop must be rebuilt or replaced. What fundamentally changes is the relationship among stocks, the value-creation capabilities carried by those stocks, and the gain functions of these relationships within the system.**

This is also the fundamental difference between case observation and conventional business case analysis. Business analysis usually focuses on what an enterprise did, why it succeeded, and which strategies are worth replicating. The New Loop Theory focuses on deeper questions:

> **What changed the fundamental relationships of value creation?**
>
> **Which stocks therefore acquired new functions?**
>
> **How did these stocks form a new Core Loop?**
>
> **How did the Core Loop acquire endogenous self-amplifying capability?**
>
> **How did existing stocks undergo migration under new system conditions?**
>
> **Ultimately, why could the new path of value accumulation become a new attractor within the system?**

These questions constitute the common language through which the theory observes cases.

Cases are neither decorative proofs of the theory nor attempts to demonstrate that every successful enterprise conforms to a standard answer. **The value of cases lies in exposing the concrete forms of theoretical mechanisms within real competitive systems, and in turn forcing the theory to accept observation at a higher resolution.**

In this sense, case observation is itself part of theoretical evolution: theory proposes mechanisms, cases provide structural feedback from complex reality, and new observations may in turn drive the theory to further refine its conceptual boundaries and causal relationships.

# 10. First Principles and New Loops

First principles help us return to more basic facts of things and re-examine those assumptions that have been taken for granted in a field.

It can help innovators discover that the product forms, business models, and competition methods we take for granted today are not necessarily the inevitable results of the problem itself.

But discovering underlying facts does not automatically mean discovering a new business loop.

First principles help answer what is fundamentally possible, while The New Loop Theory further asks: 

> **"Based on these underlying facts, can a different value creation and accumulation mechanism be formed?"**

Starting from first principles, innovators may discover an underlying capability that was previously overlooked. But only when this capability is embedded in new causal relationships and begins to generate continuous feedback can it become part of a new loop.

---

# 11. New Loops and Moats

Moats are usually used to describe an enterprise's ability to maintain a competitive advantage over the long term.

From the perspective of The New Loop Theory, a moat is not a static asset unrelated to loops. Truly powerful moats often come from stocks formed after long-term operation and the mutual reinforcement among these stocks.

Users, data, brands, technological capabilities, channels, and ecosystem partners can all become part of a moat, but the reason they form protection is that they are embedded in a continuously operating value loop.

This also means that moats are not permanently unchanging.

When a new loop changes system conditions, the stocks corresponding to the old moat may still exist, but their gain functions change. Resources that could form strong protection in the past may no longer have the same value in the new system.

From this perspective, what truly needs attention is not "whether an enterprise has a moat," but: **Which loop is this moat attached to, and are the system conditions of this loop changing?**

# 12. Theoretical Boundaries

Not all innovation models or products belong to the category of the “New Loop” as described by The New Loop Theory. A product may be better, faster, or cheaper, may possess entirely new functions, and may even bring significant user growth. However, as long as it still operates within the existing logic of value creation and accumulation, it cannot be included in the New Loop merely because it is “new.”

Similarly, new technologies, new products, new functions, new business models, and even rapid growth cannot by themselves prove the formation of a “New Loop” as understood in The New Loop Theory. What remains decisive is whether a structurally different mechanism of value creation and accumulation has formed and begun to establish its own feedback relationships.

## 12.1 The New Loop Theory and Incremental Innovation

Incremental innovation typically occurs within an existing loop, with the goal of improving the efficiency, experience, performance, or economics of the existing value creation mechanism.

The New Loop Theory, on the other hand, focuses on a new loop capable of forming a different value creation and accumulation mechanism.

Therefore, incremental innovation serves to strengthen the old loop, whereas The New Loop Theory focuses on whether there exists another loop worth establishing.

This distinction does not imply that incremental innovation is of lesser value. For mature enterprises, the continuous optimization of the existing loop is often a necessary condition for maintaining the normal operation of the business system.

The New Loop Theory addresses a different class of questions: when all participants are continuously optimizing along the same competitive dimension, does there exist a different structure that can form an alternative path for value accumulation?

## 12.2 Why Growth Does Not Equal Amplifying

Growth typically describes the increase of a flow or a variable within a loop over time.

Amplifying in the 3A framework focuses on whether this change will in turn reinforce the loop that produces it.

External capital, subsidies, resource injection, or manual expansion can all manufacture growth. However, if this growth does not translate into the genuine accumulation of stocks within the loop, and fails to form an internal feedback that can in turn reinforce the conditions of the loop, then growth itself cannot prove that the loop is Amplifying.

## 12.3 Why Growth Does Not Mean Ascendant

Growth only describes changes in scale.

**Ascendant** in the 3A framework describes the change in the system's dominant value accumulation path. A new product can grow at high speed yet still be attached to the old loop; a business may also grow very fast in a local market but has not formed a force capable of changing the system's attractor.

Therefore, growth itself is insufficient to prove being ascendant.

## 12.4 Key Stock Does Not Equal Core Stock

Key Stock is an analytical concept used to identify important targets of accumulation.

Core Stock, on the other hand, requires further demonstration: there must be a core causal relationship between this stock and the unique value creation capability of the new loop, and its continuous accumulation must be able to support the self-amplifying mechanism of the new loop.

Therefore, a Key Stock can be important, but it is not necessarily a Core Stock.

## 12.5 Core Loop Does Not Equal New Loop

A New Loop describes a complete new mechanism of value creation and accumulation.

A Core Loop describes the feedback structure within it that bears the primary role in value accumulation.

A New Loop can contain multiple reinforcing loops and balancing loops, and may also form different core accumulation relationships at different stages.

Therefore, a Core Loop cannot be simply understood as another name for a New Loop.

# 13. Relationship with Related Existing Theories

The New Loop Theory does not attempt to replace existing innovation and strategy theories. It focuses more on a mechanism that has not been fully explained among these theories: how a new loop forms, and how the value accumulation mechanism within the new loop ultimately changes the system's dominant value accumulation path.

**Christensen's "Disruptive Innovation"** mainly explains how certain new entrants enter from specific market positions and change the competitive structure under specific conditions. The New Loop Theory further focuses on how the value creation loop itself forms, and how the new loop changes system conditions.

**First Principles** help innovators return to more underlying facts and assumptions. The New Loop Theory pushes this underlying understanding further forward to value generation and feedback structures.

**Lean Startup** focuses on how to reduce the verification cost of innovation assumptions. The New Loop Theory asks a step further: what direction is actually worth being verified, and what kind of value relationships may form a new loop. **Asymmetric Iteration** further focuses on the fact that the object being adjusted during the iteration process is not only product assumptions, but potentially the value creation and accumulation structure itself.

**Design Thinking** emphasizes user needs, experience, and value creation. The New Loop Theory further focuses on whether these value relationships can form different systemic feedback.

**The Second Curve** emphasizes new growth curves outside the existing growth curve. The New Loop Theory attempts to explain the causal structure behind this new curve, and the systemic relationship between the old and new loops.

**Strategy Theories** focus on how enterprises choose competitive fields, establish unique positions, and gain long-term advantages. The New Loop Theory pushes the problem further to the loop level where competitive advantages are produced: in what loop the enterprise is actually competing, and whether there exists another loop capable of forming a different value accumulation mechanism.

These theories can complement each other, but they are not different expressions of the same theoretical problem.

# 14. Areas of Focus and Theoretical Limitations

## 14.1 Not an Organizational Change Theory

While the New Loop Theory focuses on new loops that may necessitate organizational change, the theory itself does not explain how an organization should be designed, governed, or how such change should be implemented.

The organization is just an important system condition in which the new loop operates.

## 14.2 Not a Business Model Design Method

Core loops and core stocks can help innovators analyze the core value accumulation mechanism within a business model based on the 3A framework, but The New Loop Theory currently does not provide a complete business model design process.

Innovators can design potential core loops and propose hypotheses about core stocks, but these designs still need to enter the real system to receive feedback.

## 14.3 Not an Innovation Process

There are multiple stages between the formation, self-amplification, and systemic substitution of a new loop.

Even if a new loop is **Asymmetric**, forms a **core loop** and **core stock**, and is **Amplifying**, it may still fail to become the new dominant path due to insufficient resources, immature timing, changes in system conditions, competitor reactions, or the emergence of other stronger loops.

Therefore, The New Loop Theory explains a possible mechanism of system evolution, not a guarantee of success.

**Asymmetric Iteration** describes a mechanism and state in which the innovator co-evolves with the complex competitive system, rather than a fixed innovation process.

It cannot be reduced to a linear "hypothesis-test-iterate-succeed" process.

## 14.4 Not a Predictive Model

The New Loop Theory describes a kind of systemic innovation pattern and its possible dynamic evolution, but it is not a predictive model.

Even if a new loop is **Asymmetric**, forms a **core loop** and **core stock**, and is **Amplifying**, it cannot be used to calculate when it will succeed, what the probability of success is, or that it will definitely become the new dominant path of the system.

Complex adaptive systems have a large number of interacting and incompletely controllable factors. The theory can help identify structural conditions, but it cannot eliminate the uncertainty of the system itself.

Innovators can actively shape the new loop through **Asymmetric Iteration**, but they cannot completely control the feedback from users, competitors, technologies, institutions, and other system participants.

Therefore, the theory can help innovators better understand the structures that may form, but it cannot eliminate the uncertainty in the innovation process.

## 14.5 Does Not Explain All Types of Innovation

The New Loop Theory only explains specific types of structural innovation.

A large number of product optimizations, incremental innovations, technological improvements, operational innovations, and organizational innovations do not need to be explained as new loops.

The value of the theory lies not in covering all innovations, but in accurately explaining the specific class of phenomena it has chosen.
