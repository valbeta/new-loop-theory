# The New Loop Theory — Changelog

This document records the theoretical evolution of **The New Loop Theory** across its public research versions.

The changelog is not a substitute for the formal theory documents. Each public version of the theory is represented by a complete versioned document in the `theory/` directory. This document records what changed between versions, the conceptual questions that motivated those changes, and the research directions carried forward into subsequent work.

The public repository is a curated research layer. Version histories therefore represent **publicly published theoretical states**, rather than a complete record of every intermediate research activity conducted in the private research repository.

---

## v1.3.1 — Core Stock Definition Clarification

**Status:** Current public version

v1.3.1 clarified the definition of **Core Stock**.

The clarification establishes that Core Stock should not be understood necessarily as a single measurable variable. Depending on the structure of a competitive system, it may constitute a compound or structural state variable accumulated through the Core Loop.

This distinction is important for separating **Core Stock** from **Key Stock**. Key Stock generally refers to a concrete and explicit stock indicator used to observe or manage accumulation within a system, while Core Stock refers to the deeper structural stock whose accumulation is generated and sustained by the Core Loop.

The clarification was accompanied by revisions to relevant companion cases, including NVIDIA CUDA and Netflix, to make the distinction more explicit.

### Research significance

The refinement strengthens the relationship between:

> Competitive System → Old Loop / New Loop → Core Loop → Core Stock

and provides a more precise basis for further research into how structural stock accumulation contributes to the persistence and expansion of a New Loop.

## v1.3.0 — Core Loop & Core Stock

v1.3.0 introduced **Core Loop** and **Core Stock** as explicit concepts within the theoretical architecture.

The theory distinguishes the broader competitive system from the central feedback structure through which a New Loop creates and accumulates value.

**Core Loop** refers to the central feedback structure through which the emerging competitive logic repeatedly reinforces itself.

**Core Stock** refers to the stock accumulated through that Core Loop, providing a structural basis for continued self-reinforcement.

This refinement established a more explicit relationship between loop dynamics and the accumulation of value within a competitive system.

### Research significance

The introduction of Core Loop and Core Stock extended the theory from identifying a New Loop toward examining the internal mechanism through which that loop can accumulate structural strength.

It also created a foundation for subsequent research into the nature, composition, and dynamics of Core Stock.

## v1.2.0 — The 3A Framework

v1.2.0 introduced the **3A Framework**:

* **Asymmetric**
* **Amplifying**
* **Ascendant**

The framework describes three necessary conditions through which a New Loop can develop and potentially alter the dominant path of value accumulation within a competitive system.

**Asymmetric** describes a structural difference in how value is created, captured, or accumulated.

**Amplifying** describes the endogenous reinforcing mechanism through which the effects of the New Loop become increasingly self-reinforcing.

**Ascendant** describes a system-level evolutionary condition in which the emerging loop gains increasing influence over the direction of value accumulation.

The framework also sharpened the distinction between apparent growth and endogenous amplification.

### Research significance

The 3A Framework provided a more structured account of the conditions under which a New Loop can move beyond an initial difference and begin to reshape the competitive system.

It should be understood as a framework for identifying necessary conditions, rather than as a predictive model of innovation success.

## v1.1.1 — Terminology & Concept Clarification

v1.1.1 focused on terminology and conceptual clarification across the theory.

The revision aligned key concepts and terminology to reduce ambiguity and improve consistency in the theoretical framework. One example was the clarification of **Value Creation** and its corresponding Chinese terminology, **价值创造**.

The changes in this version were primarily conceptual and terminological rather than structural.

### Research significance

The refinement established a more stable conceptual vocabulary for subsequent development of the theory and prepared the framework for deeper structural additions in later versions.

## v1.1.0 — Stock Migration & Sequential Ascendance

v1.1.0 introduced **Stock Migration** and **Sequential Ascendance** as important mechanisms for understanding how dominant value accumulation paths can change over time.

**Stock Migration** describes the system-level migration of accumulated value and resource flows between different paths of value accumulation.

The concept extends the analysis beyond a simple substitution relationship between an old competitive force and a new one. It focuses instead on how accumulated value can progressively move across competing or emerging paths as system conditions change.

**Sequential Ascendance** describes the repeated emergence and ascendance of new dominant value accumulation paths as the conditions of a competitive system evolve.

The detailed treatment of these concepts and their companion cases was incorporated into the corresponding sections of the formal theory.

### Research significance

This version expanded the theory from the emergence of a New Loop toward the broader evolution of competitive systems over time.

It established the basis for examining innovation not simply as the replacement of one product, technology, or competitor by another, but as a possible migration of the system's dominant path of value accumulation.

## v1.0 — On-ramping Foundation

**Initial public research version**

v1.0 established the initial conceptual foundation of **The New Loop Theory**.

The theory examines how a new competitive loop can emerge within an existing commercial system, gain momentum, and potentially alter the direction of value generation and accumulation.

The initial framework introduced the core theoretical vocabulary of the research, including:

* Commercial systems as complex adaptive systems
* The Loop as a fundamental unit of analysis
* Old Loop and New Loop
* Competitive Loops
* Stock and accumulation
* The three conditions of a New Loop
* The relationship between innovation and competitive system evolution
* Theoretical boundaries and relationships with related theories

The v1.0 publication also established the initial research scope, companion cases, and theoretical limitations of the framework.

The public release was accompanied by the initial **On-ramping** document intended to provide an accessible entry point into the theory.

### Research significance

v1.0 established the foundation from which subsequent versions developed a progressively more explicit account of how New Loops emerge, reinforce themselves, accumulate structural stock, and participate in changes to dominant value accumulation paths.

# Research Direction

The following research directions represent questions carried forward from the development of the theory. They are research agendas rather than claims already established by a formal public version.

## Core Stock

Further research is required to clarify the nature and structure of Core Stock.

In particular, the research needs to examine when Core Stock can be represented by a relatively explicit stock variable and when it should instead be understood as a compound or structural state variable.

A further objective is to develop a more rigorous framework for identifying, decomposing, and testing Core Stock across different types of competitive systems.

The **PACT** framework is being explored as part of this next stage of research.

## Asymmetric

Further work is needed to sharpen the definition of **Asymmetric** and distinguish structurally meaningful asymmetry from ordinary competitive advantage or superficial differentiation.

The research should examine what kinds of asymmetry can create fundamentally different value accumulation dynamics and under what system conditions such asymmetry becomes consequential.

## Amplifying

Further research is required to distinguish endogenous amplification from ordinary growth.

The central question is how a New Loop can contain a reinforcing mechanism that causes its own accumulation dynamics to strengthen over time, rather than simply benefiting from external market expansion.

## Gain Function and Attractor Migration

The theory requires further development around changes in the **Gain Function** of a competitive system and the resulting migration of its dominant attractor.

This line of research concerns how changes in system conditions can alter which loops are capable of accumulating value, and how an emerging loop can move from a peripheral position toward increasing systemic influence.

## Case System and Counterexamples

The theory will continue to be developed through a broader case system.

Future research should include not only cases that appear consistent with the theory, but also counterexamples and boundary cases that can test where the theory does not adequately explain observed outcomes.

The purpose is to strengthen the explanatory boundaries and falsifiability of the framework rather than to maximize the number of successful cases it appears to explain.

## Theoretical Boundaries

Future versions should continue to clarify the conditions under which the theory applies, the phenomena it is intended to explain, and the phenomena it should not be expected to explain.

A central research principle remains:

> The value of a theory does not lie in how many successful cases it can explain, but in whether it can clearly identify what it should explain, what it should not explain, and under what conditions it may be wrong.

# Versioning Principle

Beginning with the v1.4.0 research cycle, the project adopts a clearer distinction between **formal theory versions** and **ongoing research directions**.

A formal theory version represents a sufficiently coherent and publicly curated theoretical state. Research ideas, unresolved questions, exploratory frameworks, and incomplete constructions should not be treated as part of the formal theory until they have been sufficiently developed and incorporated into a subsequent version.

The public repository therefore distinguishes between:

**Theory Document**
A complete statement of the theory at a specific public version.

**Changelog**
A record of theoretical evolution across public versions.

**Research Direction**
Open questions and ongoing research that may contribute to future versions but do not yet constitute established theory.

The private research repository may contain intermediate hypotheses, experiments, alternative formulations, and working versions that are not published as part of the public theoretical record.
